# Blend maps

One row's value across the pad, under each law: the maps, the pins, the clamp and a cross-section. The morph editor lab's Waypoints pane (B235, B268, B272), the part the human liked: the detailed maps with the TPS·Δ and TIN·Δ options and the waypoints.

**Looks** Three glasses and a little chrome.
- **The pad** (square, up to 300 px): the selected law's value as brightness in `scr-physics` over `scr-ground`, **faint contour lines** every tenth of the range in `scr-grid` (a crease shows as a kink), the **corners** as chips in their own fills (`cA`…`cD`, edged `mark-edge`) each with its glyph and letter, A ◆, B ▲, C ●, D ■, and the **pins**. A pin is a diamond in `scr-value`, numbered, with a **value tag** (`2 91%` and an up-down grip) that is also its value handle. **TIN's triangulation** is drawn dashed in `scr-grid` for the TIN laws; every dashed line is a crease. The cross-section's line is dashed in `scr-marker` with its two ‹ › ends.
- **The maps**: one mini per law, same drawing without the tags, under a name, a one-line trait and three measured lines (how far it differs from the default law, how much of the pad is clipped, how much is outside the values set). The selected law's mini has the ink outline; a mini is a button that selects its law. A switch, **The maps show**, turns the minis into **difference from the default law**: `scr-value` where a law sits above it, `scr-meter` below, one scale for all of them.
- **Hatches**: `alarm` "/" where the value would leave the **parameter's range** (always clamped: safety, not taste); `caution` "\" where it leaves **the values you set** (the four corners and the pins): flattened when the clamp is on, heard when it is off.
- **The cross-section** (glass, 230 px): x is the position along the pad's line, y the parameter's own value, with **every law on one plot**: the range limits in `alarm`, the values you set dashed in `caution`, today's blend (no pins) dotted in `scr-grid`, each law's raw curve thin and dotted where a clamp cut it with the cut region shaded (`caution` for the set values, `alarm` for the range), each law's heard curve on top (the law in use thickest). One ink per law with a dash pattern behind it: TPS·Δ `scr-value` solid, TIN `scr-marker` solid thin, TIN·Δ `scr-physics` dashed, IDW·Δ `scr-meter` dotted. The pins on the line and the corners it passes (glyph and letter in `scr-physics`: no corner hues inside a glass, ADR-259 r4) are marked.
- **The chrome** is the house's own: the Law pills, the **Clamp to set values** switch (on by default), the pin buttons, Add pin, Delete pin, and Through the pins / A → D for the section line.

**The laws, as ruled (B272)** The setting offers two laws for 1.0: **TPS·Δ with the clamp on (the default)** and **TIN·Δ**. The lab's other two, **TIN** (plain) and **IDW·Δ**, are not options: `BlendMaps(el, { laws: ['tps', 'tind'], compare: [...] })` takes the ruled laws as the setting and any `compare` laws only as labelled comparisons (a dashed card marked COMPARISON, a "(comparison)" key entry, never selectable). The preview passes `compare: ['tin', 'idw']`; a consumer passes none.

**The Δ form** (the lab's own finding) interpolates the pin's value minus what today's bilinear law gives there, and adds it back onto today's law. So no pin is exactly today's blend, and a first pin set to what you hear changes nothing (property P5, checked). The clamp holds the heard value to the range of the values the player set, and the parameter's own range always applies. **Corners are read, never computed**: at the four exact corners the base law is returned and no clamp acts, so a pin can never change a corner's value.

**Gestures** (the lab's, :3973-4090)
- **A pin is dragged by the lab's rule, not the house relative-drag rule.** The pin follows the pointer absolutely (the lab's `pinMove` writes the pointer position); a grab within the hit radius can therefore move it by up to that radius. The house relative rule is kept for sliders; the puck, the pin and the section line's ends are positions on the pad, which the lab places absolutely. A move onto a corner (within 0.04) or another pin (within 0.04) is **refused and writes nothing**: the pin stops at its last legal spot and the reason is printed under the pad.
- Drag a pin's **tag** (or alt-drag the pin) up or down for its value: the pad's height is the parameter's range, shift ×0.1. Alt + wheel on a pin turns its value (a plain wheel scrolls the page, B301).
- Double-click empty pad adds a pin at **the value the law already gives there**, so adding one changes nothing until its value is moved. Right-click a pin deletes it. At most 8 pins per row (the lab's cap).
- Drag a ‹ › end of the dashed line to cut elsewhere; Through the pins resets it to the two most distant pins, A → D to the diagonal.
- Keyboard: each pin has a button; select one, then the arrow keys on the pad move it (1 %, shift fine), alt + ↑ ↓ turn its value, Delete removes it. Add pin places one at the first free spot of a fixed list and takes the law's value there.
- The lab's hit radius is 11 px; here it is 14 so a target is the house 28 px, and a tag's hit box is at least 28 px tall.

**The consumer provides** the row, `{ name, min, max, fmt?, corners: [A, B, C, D], pins: [[x, y, v], ...] }` (pad units 0..1, A top left, B top right, C bottom left, D bottom right; pins in stored order, which is part of the row's state: it breaks the triangulation's ties and fixes the TPS solve's rounding), and `onChange({ type, law, clamp, pins, selected })`. The call returns `{ law(), setLaw(l), clamp(), setClamp(b), row(), setRow(r), line(a, b), stats(law?), value(law, x, y), render() }`. The law functions are exposed as `BlendMaps.math` (`surface`, `at`, `triangulate`, `weights`, `laws`, `name`), so a consumer's tests run the same functions the pictures do. **The engine owns the law**: the audio side preallocates the pin slots and evaluates, the TPS solve is an edit-time solve on the main thread; this reference is the picture, not the runtime.

**Fed by** the morph layer's row: its four corner values and its pins. Pins are a proposal (the lab's `morphWaypoints` chunk, append-only grammar `L:1` first); the stored order and the caps (8 per row, 64 per patch) are the lab's. A STEPPED or gate row takes no pins; a QUANTUM patch holds them dormant: the consumer simply does not mount this component for those rows.

**Voice-bound or audio-capable** Audio-capable: it reads parameter values, not notes, so the FX device uses it as it is (a rack module's continuous row has corners and pins like any other).

**Screens** the maps, the pad and the cross-section are drawn in a glass: the screen roles (`scr-ground`, `scr-physics`, `scr-value`, `scr-meter`, `scr-marker`, `scr-grid`) follow the Screen scheme, with `alarm` and `caution` for the clamp's two hatches and the corner chips in their own fills (a chip is not part of the screen's colour set, as in the Quantum pad).

**Reduced motion** nothing animates: the pictures redraw when a pin, the law, the clamp, the maps mode, the section line, the box or the theme changes, and at no other time.

**Fits** the pad takes its column (up to 300 px); the minis wrap in a grid of 124 px or wider; the cross-section and the notes take the full width. Everything is `min-width: 0`, and the glasses scale to their box, so the component fits a 320 px card without a horizontal scroll. The notes and stat lines keep fixed boxes so a law change rewrites words, never sizes.

**Placeholders**
- A pin's mark is the lab's diamond, and corner A's glyph is also a diamond; they are told apart by ink (`scr-value`, numbered) and by the corner chips. If the human wants the two to differ in shape, that is a ruling. Decided by: the human.
- B232 (the off-corner rule), the lab's "puck" and its hover cross, the global "stations" view of every row's pins, the lab's reach and "Nth pin" measurements per law, and the self-check table are not carried: B232 is proposed and not built, the puck belongs to the Quantum pad, the others are the lab's own audit. Decided by: B232 and the morph layer's specification.
- Where pins live in the plugin state (the lab's `morphWaypoints`) and the per-row and per-patch caps are the lab's proposal until the morph layer's specification rules them.
- The sample row's range and format: a real row's come from its manifest key.
