Q1 · Depth modulation — the answer this lab proposes

A depth route is an ordinary route whose destination is another route's depth. Recursion: no — one level.
Wired — + DEPTH on any route row, a right-click on that route's depth slider (the human's right-click ruling, extended to the depth — B70), or a right-click on a lit grid cell. It arrives on ENV 1, like any sent route, and its source is re-aimed in the row. Shown — a depth is a parameter, so it is shown the way every modulated parameter already is: the violet band and live tick on the route's own depth slider (gui2's slider halo); the knob ring breathes inside a dashed envelope of its possible reach; the wire swells behind a valve the depth wire lands on; the grid cell takes gui2's dashed violet modulated ring; the trace's reach band widens. Arithmetic — still SUM, two fixed passes in one tick (depth routes, then value routes reading the modulated depths); a depth route speaks its target's units (semitones on a pitch route); the moved depth is clamped to the route's own ±range (OQ-30: the destination bounds the value). Why one level — letting a depth route target a depth route means routes read routes, which admits cycles (A deepens B, B deepens A). The instrument would then need an evaluation order and FOUNDATIONS OQ-23's unit delay — one route silently reading the previous tick of another — for a three-way product of sources nobody has asked for. One level forbids cycles by construction, keeps evaluate() at two passes with no graph sort, and covers every case named: vibrato that blooms, tremolo on the wheel. Lift it only when someone asks for depth-of-depth. Try it: the dimmed + DEPTH on an indented row refuses and says so. A port trap found on the way — mod_core's removeRoute() COMPACTS the table, so an index-keyed depth route would silently re-aim at its neighbour after a delete (the ADR-138 index-shift family). This lab's removeRoute deletes a route's depth routes with it and re-points the rest; the port needs the same.

Q2 · Visual intuition — four treatments, yours to choose

Each answers one question: what is moving this knob, by how much, right now?
A · Arcs on the knob — gui2's halo grown up: one concentric ring per route, the route's reach as a violet arc from your value, a dot where that route is pushing now, the tag in the dial's gap. Nothing new to learn; crowds past four routes. B · Wires — a patch bay: width is depth, brightness is the source's live value, dashed is inverted, a depth route is a wire landing on a wire. Reads the topology fastest; says least about the value. C · Grid — the FX-matrix well you already know: every possible route visible at once, empty cells are one click from a route. Best at "what COULD move this"; weakest at "where is it now". D · Trace — the value over time against its reach band. The only one that shows motion honestly, and the only one that can show the 172 Hz tick (250 ms view). One finding before you choose: source-coloured arcs are not available without a ruling. Every hue here has one job (magenta = your value, violet = modulation, cyan = measurement), and a per-source hue is a categorical palette the design system does not have. So source identity rides tag + ring order. If you want hue, that is an ADR-116 amendment, not a lab choice.

Q3 · Coherence as a source (B253) — what this lab proposes

Coherence is a per-note source. Where the destination is per-note, read the note's own R; where it is global, read the gain-weighted mean. Feeding it back into Coupling is stable, but it is not tame.
What R does (measured here, horde's mean-field law): a struck note starts at R = 1 because the phases retrigger at 0, then the law decides. Positive Coupling pulls it back up and, above about K 0.75 (0.74 with 5 members, 0.76 with 7), locks it to a steady value (0.80 at 0.75, 0.965 at +1). Negative Coupling splays it toward 0 (0.046 at −1 with 4 members). In between, it wanders. Detune sets how fast it wanders (at C3 with 7 members and K 0: 1, 3 and 7 peaks a second at detune 0.1, 0.28 and 0.6) but not where it locks, because horde scales coupling by the spread (σ, swarm_core.h:1889). Higher notes wander faster (8 a second at E4) because the detune is in cents. So three held notes have three different R. The rule, recommended: per-voice where the destination can be per-note. Coupling, Coherence → Tone and level are B34 Tier 1; there, route through the kPerNote scope mod_core already carries. For global destinations, use GAIN-WEIGHTED (Σ gain·R / Σ gain), which is the coherence of what you hear. Each note moves it by exactly its share of the sound. It has no hand-over jump. A releasing note fades out of it instead of dropping out. The alternatives each break one case. LOUDEST jumps when the loudest note changes: 0.235 at the chord's 1.07 s hand-over, and it ignores two thirds of the chord. NEWEST is a strike detector: it jumps to 1 on every note-on (0.897). MAX shares that fault: any fresh note pins it near 1 (0.731). It is the shell's rule for envelopes, where it is right, but it is wrong for R. MEAN counts a near-silent tail the same as the lead (0.374). One honest caveat: GAIN-WEIGHTED also steps at a strike when the attack is shorter than a tick (0.321 here, 5 ms attack), but its step is bounded by the new note's gain share. The feedback case — Coherence 1 → OSC 1 Coupling, through the one-tick (5.8 ms) delay. The loop is stable by construction because R is bounded to 0..1 and K is clamped to ±1. The delay changes nothing measurable: the same law with 0, 1, 4 and 16 ticks of delay gives the same picture, because the swarm's own time is hundreds of ms. The sign is what matters. Positive depth latches. Locking raises Coupling, which holds the lock (+0.5 from base 0.3 → R 0.97, K pinned at +1). It has hysteresis: it holds with the base turned down as far as 0 and lets go only below about −0.1. At a smaller depth it bursts: the swarm catches, holds for a moment and lets go (+0.25 from 0.3 → R swings 0.03 ↔ 0.93, irregularly). Negative depth is a governor. Rising R lowers Coupling, so the swarm can never lock (base 0.75 alone locks at R 0.80; with −0.5 it wanders 0.06…0.61). Both are musically useful: the burst is the most alive thing on this page. But "latch" means a patch can sit coherent for the whole note. A re-strike does NOT free it, because RETRIG starts every note at R = 1, already inside the latch. Only turning the base down does, and a player must be able to see that.

A · Arcs on the knob chrome · drag a knob to move its base

What — tag per ring, in the dial's gap. How much — ring length (solid = reach at today's depth; dashed = reach the depth modulation can open). Now — the dot per ring and the violet tick across the track for the sum; amber when the destination clamps. Cost: crowds past ~4 routes; the rings are per-route, so the eye has to add them; and the knob's scale is the PARAMETER's — on a ±48 st pitch knob a 0.7 st vibrato is a sliver (see OSC 1 Pitch), which the trace, autoscaled, does not suffer.

B · Wires tube · click a destination

What — follow the wire. How much — wire width is the depth NOW (it breathes when modulated); a depth route lands on the ◇ valve at the wire's middle. Now — wire brightness is the source's value; the destination bar shows base (magenta) and now (lime/teal). Cost: reads topology, not magnitude — two wires of equal width can mean ±1 or ±48 st.

C · Grid well · click empty = add · drag = depth · right-click = + depth

What — row × column. How much — the magenta fill is the depth you set (amber = negative). Now — the violet floor bar. A dashed violet ring = this cell's depth is itself modulated. Cost: 20 × 9 cells to show ~6 routes; duplicates (two routes, one cell) show as ×2 and sum.

D · Trace tube · click a lane

What — lane per routed destination (the ledger below names the routes). How much — the violet band is the reach at the depth in force at that instant, so depth modulation shows as the band breathing. Now — the right edge. Each lane autoscales to what moved in view and prints its scale. The 250 ms view draws the value as the staircase it is: one step per 5.8 ms control tick (B199 axis 5). Cost: needs a second of history to be read at all.

What is moving it, right now

The arithmetic, in the order the core does it: depth routes add to their target's depth (clamped to that route's ±range); each route contributes depth-now × mapped-source; contributions SUM; the destination's own range clamps last. Cutoff is modulated in Hz, linearly — ADR-136's base + delta·span on a log knob — so a bipolar LFO sweeps further up than down in pitch terms. That is today's shell, reproduced, not endorsed; it is not this lab's question.
