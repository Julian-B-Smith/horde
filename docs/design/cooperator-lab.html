Cloud mode

One carrier at the note; modulators detuned copies of one modulator at ratio (log₂ slider: 0.25×..8×), Kuramoto-coupled among THEMSELVES. Locked, they sum to a single modulator — classic 2-op FM. Unlocked, the modulation is chorused and smeared. The normalisation is Σsin/M, so an unlocked cloud also modulates less — coherence physically gates the index.

Network mode

Op 1 is the carrier at the note; r2–r4 are the others' ratios (log₂). The grid is the "algorithm" — cell i←j is how strongly op j drives op i, and it is CONTINUOUS, so morphing between DX-style algorithms is a drag, not a menu. edge law is the experiment: at 0 every edge is a Kuramoto edge (j pulls i's frequency toward phase agreement — gentle, self-tuning); at 1 every edge is an FM edge (j's output injected into i's phase — strong, timbral). In between, both. Mutual FM normally goes chaotic immediately; the Kuramoto share plus the gravity basin is what makes it playable. mix blends ops 2–4 into the output alongside the carrier.

Honest physics note: the R meter reads LOW in network mode (measured 0.2–0.3) because the four ops sit at DIFFERENT frequencies, and a 1:1 phase pull cannot lock a 2:1 ratio — so Kuramoto edges matter most between near-unison ops, or after gravity has captured two ratios onto the same lattice point. True cross-ratio locking needs n:m edges (sin(n·θj − m·θi), Arnold tongues) — queued as increment 2, not faked here. Also by design: a Kuramoto edge INTO op 1 bends the note's pitch toward agreement (measured max 33.5 ¢, clamped at ±80) — mutual coupling taken seriously; zero op 1's row to silence it.
