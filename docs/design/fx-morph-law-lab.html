Corner A routes source → DRIVE → COMB → out. Corner B routes the same two modules the other way: source → COMB → DRIVE → out. Both corners are the same modules with the same settings — only the order differs. The question this lab exists to settle is what should happen between them, and it is a question you answer by ear, not by argument. Rule on the law, not on the preset: try it on the drone and on the pluck, because sustained and transient material disagree about this.

The morph

ADR-031 — the feedback law

—

Our decided audio feedback law (ADR-031, promoted from two measured bug post-mortems): (a) feedback paths normalise /N assuming worst-case correlation — a √N norm was measured unstable above regen 0.35 — while output paths take /√N; (b) a DC blocker inside every loop, corner well clear of the lowest musical comb. Both are Layer-0 guarded (L0-19/L0-21) so they cannot silently regress. Rev 1 of this lab obeyed neither, which is why BLEND howled. Flip the control to hear the difference — and note the runaway-then-squash is the exact phenomenology ADR-031 already documents.

Modules

Deliberately strong defaults: a subtle drive and a shy comb make every law sound identical, which would be a false negative rather than a result.

Output

—

Rev 1 of this panel claimed BLEND ran +9 dB hot and clipped. That measurement is RETRACTED — it was measuring a runaway cycle, not blending, because rev 1 obeyed none of ADR-031's feedback law. With the law applied the loop is bounded (loop gain ≈ 0.06 at the defaults) and BLEND's midpoint sits a few dB above corner A, which is the honest parallel-sum figure. Two further cautions, both learned here: a single analyser buffer is 46 ms and this source is seven detuned saws whose beat period is seconds, so short-window level readings drifted 9.4 dB on identical states (2.5 s averaging brings it to 1.2); and the coefficient normalisation is verified at the matrix (outputs ×0.707, feedback ×0.500, exactly as the law says) rather than inferred from a level difference.

The scope is orientation, not evidence. A meter cannot tell you whether the middle of the sweep is musical; only listening can. Judge with your ears and use the trace only to confirm something is actually reaching the output.
