horde bend lab

Bench for pitch-bend inertia (human request 2026-08-03) — pitch does not arrive where you point it, it travels there. Five travel laws, because "how long does a glide take" has more than one honest answer and they do not sound alike: constant time takes the same duration near or far, constant rate holds a fixed cents-per-second so distance sets the duration, lag is asymptotic and never technically arrives, and only the spring is inertia in the literal sense — it can overshoot and ring, because a mass in motion does not stop when the force does. The bench measures each: step response with lag / overshoot / settle / reversals, plus a vibrato test, because the price of inertia is that it eats fast wheel vibrato and that cost should be a number, not a surprise. Both lanes are live — the bend wheel and note pitch (where the spring puts a blip on every onset, the way a struck resonator does), with per-note MPE bend as a toggle. Keys A..;, Z/X octave, space drone, drag the wheel, or fire a gesture (identical input = a fair A/B, which a hand-dragged wheel can never give you).

Inertia model

Only the knobs your model reads are live. The first three are the three honest ways to answer "how long does a glide take": constant time takes the same duration near or far, constant rate takes the same cents-per-second so distance sets the duration, and lag is asymptotic — it approaches forever and never technically arrives, which is why it has no "glide time" at all.

dist→overshoot: a plain spring already overshoots by a fixed percentage, so absolute overshoot is already proportional to how far you moved — that is 1, the natural law. The knob bends it: overshoot ¢ ∝ distance^k. 0 = the same absolute wobble whatever you play; 2 = only the big sweeps bounce. return × scales the time when heading back toward zero (a real spring snaps home faster than you can push) — bend lane only, since a note has no home. ζ: 1 = critical (fastest arrival, no overshoot), <1 rings, >1 sluggish.

Wheel & gestures

tap is the diagnostic one: an instant there-and-back that no hand can perform. A lag just rounds it off; a spring keeps moving after your hand has stopped, which is the whole point of the model. dive is −12 st and back — the case where a rate limit and a lag part company completely.

Swarm

No coupling in this bench on purpose: K does not touch the pitch lane, and leaving it out means nothing else can be blamed for what you hear. Detune and drift stay because bend on a swarm is the case that matters — a moving pitch drags 7 slightly-different pitches with it.
