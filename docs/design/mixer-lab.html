The MIX page as a signal-chain instrument. Every stage from each source's gain to the master carries a meter with a peak hold and a CLIP lamp that latches until you click it: the three sources (after their gain, after B209's pre-mix filter, and — for the swarms — after the core's own output saturator), the mix sum, each FX slot's input and output, and the master. DRIVE makes the lab's own chain clip at the stage you pick; the lamp lights there and at nothing upstream, and the page proves that at load for every stage (line below). Every meter says whether the shell can feed it today — FED, PART or OWED — from the table at the foot of the page, which cites file:line for both.

FIRST OVER — none latched
HOT multiplies the signal at that tap for as long as it is chosen. SPIKE replaces ONE sample there with ±1.12 (+1 dB) every 3000–7000 samples (seeded). FEED chooses how the meters are fed: STICKY — the maximum over every block since the last read, which the port owes; LAST BLOCK — what the shell's publish does today (a fresh snapshot every 128-sample block, read once per 768-sample frame, so five blocks in six are never seen). Put SPIKE on any stage and flip FEED to watch a real over go missing.

Design notes and what is left for the human

1 · WHICH ROW IS THE PAGE?Eighteen meter pairs is a lab, not a page. The lab's lean: lamps at every tap (cheap, and the only thing that answers "where"), full meters on the three sources, the mix and the master, and each FX slot's in/out as the compact level-history strip under its card, which shows what the slot does to level over time (COMP pulling down, ECHO building up), not only whether it went over. Your call.
2 · A SWARM'S OVER IS BEFORE ITS SATURATORThe swarm core ends in tanh (swarm_core.h:1329-1332), so the one source meter the shell publishes (oscPeak, post-gain, post-tanh) can never exceed 1.0 and its clip class can never fire. The last place a swarm's level is still a number above 1.0 is the FILTER tap, before that tail. Is driving the core's own tanh a "clip" to warn about, or part of the sound? The lab lamps it, because it is where level turns into distortion; a SAT label instead of CLIP on that lamp would be a one-word change.
3 · THE LATCH LIVES ON THE AUDIO THREADA lamp can only latch what the GUI is shown. The shell's publish (hypersaw_clap.cpp:8176, every block, into a double buffer at :5086-5087) hands the GUI the newest block; everything between two reads is overwritten. Put DRIVE on SPIKE and flip FEED to LAST BLOCK: most spikes never reach a lamp (the audit line counts them). The port owes a sticky reading per tap — max since the last read plus an over count, OR'd across blocks and cleared by the reader. The lamp's latch and its click-to-clear can then live in the GUI.
4 · "THE MIX SUM" IS A MATRIX GATHERhorde has no mix bus. In the default topology every source gathers into slot 1 at 1.0 (routing_core.h:113-128), so MIX · SUM and FX 1 · IN carry the same samples until a crosspoint moves. Crosspoints run −2..+2 (hypersaw_clap.cpp:1132-1135), so a slot input CAN go over from routing alone: FX n · IN is metered after the edge coefficient, and the lab's in row per slot IS that coefficient. Keep a separate SUM lamp, or let FX 1 · IN be it?
5 · A LIMITER HIDES EVERY OVER ABOVE ITThe default rack ends in COMP, whose 0.98 brickwall (fx_rack.h:707-708) is always on. Clip at FX 2 and the master meter stays clean — which is the whole case for lamps before the last slot: the master alone cannot tell you the rack is overdriven.
6 · FLOAT OVERS ARE NOT YET DAMAGEInside a float engine a sample above 1.0 survives; what an intermediate lamp reports is where level went hot, which matters because the next nonlinear stage (drive, the core's tanh, the comp's brickwall, the host's output) will act on it. Only the MASTER over is clipping in the DAC sense. The lamp reads the same everywhere on purpose.
