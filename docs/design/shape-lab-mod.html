The modulator editor as one lab. An LFO and an envelope, both drawn and edited as breakpoints, and both evaluated by one shape law, shapeAt(points, t). The picture and the audio both call it. HEAR switches what you listen to between the PROPOSED laws (the three panels in the middle row) and the SHIPPED ones (B171, as src/hypersaw_clap.cpp runs them). Whichever you are not hearing is drawn as a dashed ghost. Press POWER, then play the keys or A..L, space = drone, Z/X = octave. Double-click any slider to reset it.

LFO · one cycle · bipolar ±1 · global, like the shipped two

SHAPES
Drag a point · double-click empty space to add one, a point to delete it (right-click deletes too) · drag the small mid-segment handle up/down to bend that segment. Two points at the same time make a vertical step, so saw and square are exact rather than steep ramps. The ends sit at 0 and 1 and are not forced equal: the wrap from the last value back to the first is a step, drawn dashed. The dashed φ line is the start phase, the solid dot is where the sound is reading right now, and the ring is where the other law would be reading. S&H and RANDOM (round 3, below) have no breakpoints: a new seeded value every cycle is not a fixed cycle. Pick one and this canvas shows eight cycles of the stream instead, with the dots on the draws. Any breakpoint shape brings the editor back.

ENVELOPE · per voice · time axis in real seconds

SHAPES
Same editor, same law. Time runs left to right in seconds, the picture is the note held until it reaches the sustain point and then released. While the key is down the envelope holds at the SUS point. On release it plays the segments after SUS, starting from whatever level it has reached, so an early release never jumps. NONE makes it one-shot: the gate only starts it. The A/D/S/R sliders write a four-point shape. Edit the points by hand and the sliders stop describing it.

1 · TEMPO DIVISION

Proposed: the stored value stays beats per cycle (lfoNBeats, the same id and the same 0.0625 to 8 range). The control becomes a named list: the 22 straight, dotted and triplet values inside that range, each written as an exact beat count. The name is UI and the beats are the truth, the same ruling as gui2's scale picker. No id moves and no patch migrates. A patch holding an off-list value loads unchanged and reads as ≈ nearest ±x%.
A SHIPPED PATCH'S CONTINUOUS BEATS → THE LIST
Why not two controls (base × straight/dotted/triplet)? That is two automation lanes for one quantity, and it can name values that do not exist (1/64 triplet is below the range). The widest gap in the list is 4:3, so no shipped value is more than ±15.5% from a name. The list and the knob above write the same stored beats, and both HEAR laws read it. The knob is the shipped control, kept here to show how any value it can hold reads against the list.

2 · START PHASE

Proposed: phase is a live offset on the readout, out = shape(frac(acc + φ)). A retrigger rewinds acc to 0, so a struck note still starts at φ (the shipped meaning, kept). Free-running, turning φ moves the wave now. In sync, acc comes from the transport (beats since bar 1 ÷ cycle beats), so φ is measured from the bar, and a division change lands back on the grid. In free mode this matches the shipped output exactly for any patch whose φ has not moved since activation.
Shipped (hypersaw_clap.cpp:2854, :3820): φ is copied into the phase at activation and at a retrigger. Free-running, it is never read again, so the knob does nothing you can hear until the plugin is re-activated. Hear it: DRONE, LFO free on CUTOFF, retrig off, then sweep φ. Under PROPOSED the sweep jumps with the knob. Under SHIPPED nothing happens until RE-ACTIVATE.
WHERE EACH LAW IS IN ITS CYCLE ON THE LAST BEAT
—
The sync half, measured. With SYNC on at 1/4, both laws read the same place on every beat. Change the division to 1/4T and back. The proposed law lands back on its phase, while the shipped accumulator (:3822, phase += bpm/60/beats · dt, no song position) keeps whatever offset the detour left it, for good.

3 · ENVELOPE TIME

Proposed: the number on a stage is when it arrives. A stage is a breakpoint segment that ends at the knob's time. Its curvature is a separate per-segment control, and c = +0.5 is the one-pole's own shape, scaled so it arrives. To keep an old patch's sound, migrate τ × ln 100 = τ × 4.61 → arrival time with c = +0.5.
Shipped (advanceAdsr, hypersaw_clap.cpp:2789): each knob is a one-pole time constant. At the stated time a stage is only 63.2% of the way there. The attack snaps to 1 past 0.99 (:2802), which takes 4.61τ. A release reaches exact zero only at the 1e-6 snap (:2807), 13.8τ from full level. Relabelling the knobs "τ" was the other option. It was rejected because it leaves the player to multiply by 4.6 in their head, and a breakpoint you drag already is an arrival time. Dashed = shipped, solid = proposed, the same four numbers, the gate released 0.3 s after the proposed sustain.

4 · OTHER LOGIC THAT WAS WRONG

  1. Mirror was not a mirror (the old version of this file: curve pow(x, e^(3c)), mirror negated c). That is only exact for an exponential. The error reached 0.41 of a segment at c = 0.6, the value the file's own ADSR preset used. The curve is now the normalised exponential, and mirror is exact (audited above).
  2. A rate labelled in seconds, read as Hz (old file: the slider said "rate / duration … s", while LFO mode computed 2 / rate seconds for two cycles). Rate is now Hz and division is beats. Neither is a duration.
  3. It made no sound (old file). Its "output" canvas was a second rendering of the picture, so nothing tested the picture against a sound. Now the HEARD lanes are taps of the render, and the audit compares them.
  4. Envelope loop clicked (modulator-lab.html:476 with :459). LOOP restarted the attack from 0 while the level sat at sustain, a step of height = sustain on every loop. Here every segment starts from the level it is actually at. Looping is the LFO's job.
  5. Two time grammars (modulator-lab.html:215-219: seconds for its envelope, normalised t for this file's editor, "unreconciled"). Here one evaluator serves both, and the unit is the caller's: cycle fraction for the LFO, seconds for the envelope.
  6. A shipped comment claims every LFO shape crosses zero at phase 0 (hypersaw_clap.cpp:2857). That is true only for sine and triangle. Saw-up starts at −1, and saw-down and square start at +1. So a retriggered saw lands its destination at full excursion. What the comment's reasoning actually needs is the zero mean, and that part is true. This is a doc fix for the lead to queue, not changed here.
  7. Synced LFOs are not bar-locked (:3822). The phase accumulates from activation, not from song position, so where the peak falls in the bar depends on when the plugin woke. After any division change, the offset is permanent. Panel 2 measures it, and the proposed law derives the phase from the transport.
  8. The B208 row says "0.912 at 1·τ". A one-pole at 1τ is 1 − e−1 = 0.632. 0.91 is what lfoenv_check's first pass read at a probe instant it does not record (tools/lfoenv_check.cpp:44-47). The lab uses 0.632 and draws it.

PRESET · what a patch would store


    
Points are [t, v, c]: t in cycles for the LFO and in seconds for the envelope, c the
      segment's curvature. Rate is Hz, or beats with the division's name stored beside it for display. Nothing
      in a shape depends on the sample rate or the tempo (ADR-009).

      KURO LFO · ORBITAL · MIDI GATE, TRIGGER AND TRACKERS: THE SAME ONE-LAW RULE
    

  




Three source families join the lab. The Kuro LFO is a Kuramoto swarm of LFOs (ADR-053). ORBITAL
is an N-body gravity field whose observables are sources (ADR-165). The MIDI trackers add a note-on gate, a
note-on trigger and six trackers. Every source is read through one table. The destination reads it there, and
the pictures read it there too, or draw history the render logged from it. ROUTES sends two continuous sources to a
level. EVENTS sends the trigger to actions. POWER, then play the keys (the key's height sets velocity), or press
PHRASE, a fixed three-second phrase with legato, a gap, staccato and a rolled chord.

KURO LFO · a Kuramoto swarm of LFOs · ADR-053 · global

    
    

    
Each voice is an LFO with its own rate (detune spreads the rates, and anchor picks which
      voice sits on the rate). The voices are coupled through the swarm's mean phase. K > 0 pulls them into a
      cluster, so the voices agree and R, their coherence, rises toward 1. K < 0 spreads them evenly round
      the circle, where R falls to 0 (the rank-lattice splay, ADR-053's A5 fix, confirmed by ear on 2026-07-24).
      K = 0 lets each voice run free. The wheel is the swarm now, and the strips are what the render logged:
      voices on top, R below. The ringed voice is the one a route is reading. DRAWN runs every voice through the
      LFO card's breakpoints, the same shapeAt as round 1. Transcribed from mod-lab.html
      (mean-field and splay only) with its arithmetic unchanged, and checked bit-for-bit against it (see the trace).

  


  
ORBITAL · N-body gravity · ADR-165 · global

    
    

    

    

    
Bodies attract under softened gravity (velocity Verlet, the reference's step()). You set up
      a system and route what it does, and never draw a shape. Sources per body are x, y (y up), speed (|v| ÷ the
      preset's own full range since round 6, ADR-375; ÷ the 3.0 clamp under SCALE = CLAMP), near (1 − the softened distance to the nearest body) and angle about the centre (0..1,
      wrapping, and not smoothed, because a smoother would sweep through 0.5 at every wrap). Per field there are
      energy and spread. The ticks on the bottom and left edges are each body's x and y, and a ticked body a
      route is reading is drawn in meter colour. Round 6 (below) adds accel, sin and cos of the angle, and the
      thermostat. Steps are counted in samples (SPEC-ORBITAL §6.2) at the lab's
      1/480 s. ADD BODY draws its position from a seeded stream, (seed, add count), so an added body is patch
      state. KICK and RESET are the spec's two trigger inputs (§7), and they are also action targets in
      EVENTS.

  


  
MIDI TRACKERS · gate · trigger · six trackers · global

    
GATE is a level: 1 while any key is held. It is legato. A note-on while it is high does
      not dip it, and a note-off leaves it high while another key is down, so it never re-attacks. Re-attacking on an
      overlapping note is the TRIGGER's job. The trigger is an event: it fires at a sample and has no value
      in between. EVERY fires on each note-on, and FIRST fires only on the note-on that opens the gate (the
      legato trigger).

    
    

    

  






  
ROUTES · a continuous source → a level, every sample

    

    
    
Two slots, each a source and a destination with a depth. The destinations use the same laws as
      the LFO card's (cutoff ±½ range, pitch ±½ octave, level down to 1 − depth). A lane shows the value the destination
      received, logged where it is applied, so the lane and the sound cannot differ. That is what the round-2
      audit checks. The TRIGGER is listed and refused. An event has no value between its ticks, so a depth has
      nothing to scale. It reaches a level only through an action in EVENTS.

  


  
EVENTS ARE NOT LEVELS · the trigger and its action targets

    
A continuous source has a value at every sample, and a route scales it. An event source
      is a count at a sample offset, and there is nothing to scale. It fires an action. Some actions hold
      state, and those turn an event back into a level: SAMPLE ON TRIGGER samples its input and holds it
      (renamed from "S&H" in round 3, so that "S&H" means one thing: the LFO shape), and MOD ENV
      restarts the envelope card's shape from the level it has reached. The rest act on a module: they restart the LFO,
      reset the Kuro swarm, or kick or reset ORBITAL. The last one posts randomize unlocked to a hosted
      module.

    

    
    

    
Lanes, top to bottom. The gate is a level. The trigger has ticks, not a line. SAMPLE ON TRIGGER is stepped,
      the event made into a level. MOD ENV is the round-1 envelope law, restarted by the trigger and released when the
      gate falls, so under FIRST an overlapping note does not restart it. The in-core actions land on the
      trigger's own sample. The hosted one cannot, because a module's patch changes on the main thread (see the next
      card).

  


  
HOSTED MODULE · a generic stand-in, not any real module

    
HORDE sends WHEN, and the module owns WHAT. The trigger crosses the module boundary as one
      message: an action name, a sequence number and the sample it fired on. The module draws from its own seeded
      stream, over its own unlocked parameters, and hands back a patch. HORDE never sees the stream or the locks.
      The draw changes the module's patch, so it runs on the main thread. The audio thread only posts into a
      preallocated ring, and the lag between firing and applying is shown below.

ROUND 2 · FINDINGS AND OPEN QUESTIONS FOR THE HUMAN · B226

    
    
      
  1. May a trigger fire a HOSTED module's action across the module boundary? The lab designs it as a
        message (randomize-unlocked, seq, fired-at sample), posted to the main thread, with the module owning
        the stream and the locks. Three sub-rulings follow if the answer is yes. (a) Latency. It is not
        sample-accurate. It lands at the next main-thread tick, a few ms to one UI frame. (b) Undo. One undo step
        per trigger floods the history in an arpeggio. Should the steps coalesce per gesture, or should the trigger be
        rate-limited (a minimum interval)? (c) Automation. The parameter changes that result: are they written
        to host automation while it records, or kept out of it?
    2. 
      
  2. Trigger default: EVERY or FIRST (legato)? The shipped LFO retrig (round 1) is exactly this trigger
        routed to LFO RESTART under EVERY. A legato retrig is the same route under FIRST, so one mode control could
        replace a per-module retrig switch.
    3. 
      
  3. A release trigger (an event on note-off, or on the gate falling) is not built. Wanted? Likewise, should the
        sustain pedal (CC64) hold the gate?
    4. 
      
  4. Scope. Every tracker here is GLOBAL (one per instrument, last-note priority). Velocity, note and time
        since note-on also make sense per voice, and the matrix already has to tell per-note sources from global
        ones (mod-lab finding #2, per-note envelopes). Which trackers get a per-voice twin?
    5. 
      
  5. Tracker ranges, stated here and not ruled. NOTE is (n − 60) ÷ 48 (C4 = 0, four octaves = full scale),
        last-note priority. Is high or low priority wanted? ORDER wraps at N (a round-robin). Is an unwrapped count
        wanted instead? SINCE resets with the trigger, so under FIRST it is the time since the phrase began. Is
        that the right reading?
    6. 
      
  6. ORBITAL's normalisation. SPEC-ORBITAL §4 normalises speed by the 3.0 clamp and energy by its maximum.
        On the shipped presets that reads about 0.1 and 0.01, too small to hear without a gain. Should they be
        normalised per preset, like the ruled fixed ceiling for accel? accel is not built, because the
        ruling fixes a per-preset ceiling but no value. Angle is unsmoothed (see the card).
    7. 
      
  7. ORBITAL's source count is still B126's open collision: up to 8 bodies × 5 observables + 2 against fixed
        matrix slots. The lab exposes bodies 1 to 4.
    8. 
      
  8. Two transcriptions, no committed gate. The Kuro swarm and ORBITAL's step are copies (neither source can
        be imported by a page). Each was checked bit-for-bit against its original in scratch (trace). Keeping them

  9. A stale charter line. CLAUDE.md §Domain still lists ORBITAL's add-body seed as outstanding. The reference

SMOOTHING · one seeded stream, five laws · click a lane to hear it

    
The stream. Draw k of mulberry32(lfoSeed) → vk = 2uk − 1, and
      lfoSeed is the engine's own: patch seed ^ 0x9E3779B9 (hypersaw_clap.cpp:2847). The LFO's position is
      round 1's p = acc + φ, not wrapped: k = ⌊p⌋ picks the draw and x = p − k is where in the cycle.
      NONE = vk (this is S&H). LINEAR vk → vk+1 across the cycle.
      COSINE the same with a half-cosine ease, so it is flat at every draw. CUBIC a Catmull-Rom through
      vk−1..vk+2, smooth through every draw, clamped to ±1. SLEW glides from wherever it is to
      vk, arriving exactly after the slew time, which is in ms and does not change with the rate.

    
    

    

    
Every lane is the same eight draws (the dots), the page the LFO is on now. The solid lane is the one
      you hear, and the meter-coloured line is where the sound is reading. The first four lanes pass through every dot at
      the cycle start. Only LINEAR, COSINE and CUBIC look one draw ahead, which a seeded stream allows. SLEW is causal:
      it trails the S&H step (drawn dashed in its lane), and when the slew is longer than a cycle it never
      arrives, and each glide starts where the last one stopped. Its curve is round 1's segment law at c = +0.5, the
      one-pole's shape, arriving at the stated time. It is not a new curve. Because SLEW carries state, its lane is
      exact for cycles already played (the core records where each glide started) and a forecast for the rest of
      the page at the current rate. The audit holds S&H and the four pure lanes to the sound, and SLEW's law but not
      its forecast.

ROUND 3 · FINDINGS AND OPEN QUESTIONS FOR THE HUMAN · B237

    
    
      
  1. The naming ruling. Is "S&H" the LFO shape and "SAMPLE ON TRIGGER" the trigger's action, as proposed
        (card above)? Or should S&H gain an input selector, so that the two become one module with two clocks?
    2. 
      
  2. The first cycle. The shipped S&H holds 0 for its first cycle after activation or a seed change. The
        proposal draws at every cycle start, the first included. Adopt it? It shifts the shipped sequence one cycle
        earlier. The numbers stay the same.
    3. 
      
  3. What a strike does to the stream. Proposed: the next unused draw, so each note gets a new step. The
        alternatives are the shipped "keep the held value" and a third option, "rewind the stream", where every struck
        note replays the same sequence from draw 0 (a repeatable random riff). Which is wanted? It could be a per-LFO
        choice.
    4. 
      
  4. Look-ahead. LINEAR, COSINE and CUBIC pass through each draw at its cycle start and head for the
        next one. That is possible only because the stream is seeded, so draw k + 1 is known. The causal alternative
        glides toward each draw after it is taken, which arrives one cycle late (what "S&H into a lag" does). SLEW
        is that causal form already. Keep the knots on the cycle starts?
    5. 
      
  5. CUBIC overshoot. A Catmull-Rom through values in ±1 can reach ±1.25 (the run −1, +1, +1, −1). The
        lab clamps to ±1, which flattens those peaks. The alternative is a cubic B-spline: it never overshoots and is
        smoother still, but it does not pass through the draws, so its range shrinks. Which one?
    6. 
      
  6. SLEW's law. Proposed: arrival time in ms along round 1's c = +0.5 curve, consistent with "the number on a
        stage is when it arrives". The alternatives are a constant-rate limiter (ms per full-scale step, so small steps
        arrive sooner) and a plain one-pole τ (never arrives). Should SLEW also apply after LINEAR/COSINE/CUBIC? Not
        built.
    7. 
      
  7. A shipped picture/sound mismatch (out of scope, src/, for the lead to queue). gui2 draws the S&H
        as the first eight draws and puts the start-phase dot on draw 0, "the value a strike at any phase holds"
        (src/gui/gui2.html:6925-6929). The engine's strike does not draw (:3949). It holds whatever
        the last wrap drew, which is 0 after activation. Draw 0 is first heard in the second cycle
        (:2859, :3954-3961).
    8. 
      
  8. STEPS is not S&H. Round 1's STEPS preset is a seeded 8-step breakpoint shape. It repeats every
        cycle. S&H never repeats. Both stay: STEPS is a drawn sequence, S&H is a random source.
    9. 
      
  9. No committed gate. The round-3 audit is in-page, like rounds 1 and 2. A tools/*_check that runs it
        headless would keep it honest, and that is the lead's call.

2-D RANDOM · the controls · one seed, three modes

    
One pipeline. A unit point c in [−1, 1]² → bias warp → shape (box, or the
      disc by an area-preserving map) → home + spread · s → fold into the pad. WALK moves c itself; JUMP and
      SCATTER draw a new c per trigger and travel to where it lands. The bias is a warp of the same draws, never a
      redraw, so the draw count is fixed (2 per Walk tick, 2 per trigger) and a replay reads the same numbers under any
      bias. Nothing clips: the walk and the pad's edges reflect, and the spring bounces off the walls.

ROUND 4 · CHOICES AND OPEN QUESTIONS · B540

    
    
      
  1. Walk's three knobs. speed is the OU time constant τ in seconds (how long the pull toward home takes to
        forget a push). range is σ, the drift's typical distance from home in units of the spread (the update is the
        OU's exact transition, so σ holds at any tick rate). smooth is two one-poles in series at smooth · τ / 2: they
        round the drift's jagged corners and cost some range. Is smoothness better as its own time, in seconds?
    2. 
      
  2. Spring. The ruling names overshoot, tension and damping. Overshoot and damping are one quantity for a
        spring (overshoot = e−πζ/√(1−ζ²), the bend lab's law), so the lab has tension (Hz) and
        damping (ζ) as knobs and shows the overshoot as their readout. Should the knob be overshoot % instead, with ζ
        derived (the bend lab's dist→overshoot does that inversion)?
    3. 
      
  3. Bias strength. Not in the ruling: a single amount control (0 = no bias at any setting). CORNERS pushes
        each axis outward, EDGES pushes the box radius out, CENTRE pulls it in. In the disc, CORNERS means the four
        diagonals on the rim.
    4. 
      
  4. The clock is the source's own. RATE and TEMPO count from the replay, not from bar 1 of the transport, so a
        replay is a true restart. A tempo-locked version (round 1's SYNC rule: phase from the bar) is the alternative.
        Which?
    5. 
      
  5. The seed is the source's own field, as SAMPLE ON TRIGGER's is, not the patch seed. Each mode has its own
        stream (seed XOR a fixed word), so switching mode changes the numbers, and a mode change replays.
    6.

IMPULSE AND ARP · a shape per trigger · a mock arp row

    
An impulse is a one-shot shape that each trigger strikes on a continuous parameter: attack, then
      decay, in seconds, on round 1's curve law. It is added through the mod matrix like any mapping (here: the mock
      Shriek · drive knob + amount × the shape), summed and clamped once. A retrigger restarts the attack
      from the level it has reached, never from 0, so it cannot click. Polarity: UNI pushes up, BI swings
      from −amount at rest to +amount at the peak, INV pulls down. CONTROL is not offered: it replaces the knob's
      position with the source's, and a shape that falls back to nothing between strikes would leave the knob ignored.

ROUND 5 · CHOICES AND OPEN QUESTIONS · B541

    
    
      
  1. The meter is transport data (4/4, 3/4, 5/4, 6/8, 7/8), read at each block's first sample like the tempo. Bar
        divisions ("1 bar", "2 bars", ...) and the offset count from the bar's real length (7/8 = 3.5 beats). Note values
        (1/4, 1/8T, ...) stay fixed in beats. A meter change mid-play recomputes the grid from the origin, so the next tick can
        land off the old grid. Should a meter change re-anchor at the next bar instead?
    2. 
      
  2. Offset. A fraction of a bar on the tempo grid and of a period in Hz. On a grid finer than the offset it is
        taken modulo a swing pair, so the first bar never loses ticks. Is offset better in 16ths only?
    3. 
      
  3. Swing moves the odd tick of each pair from 50% to up to 75% of the pair. Should it apply to grids of a bar
        or more? It does here.
    4. 
      
  4. NOTE origin runs while a key is held and restarts at every note-on (the round-2 TRIGGER, so the MIDI card's
        EVERY or FIRST decides what counts as a note-on). Alternatives: run until the next note-on, or for a set count.
    5. 
      
  5. Probability is per tick index, so under NOTE origin every note replays the same dice from tick 0. That makes
        a repeatable riff; a stream that continues across notes is the alternative.
    6. 
      
  6. A free Hz rate counts from the play (or the note-on), not from page load, so it starts with the transport.
    7. 
      
  7. BI on an impulse rests at −amount (the bipolar meaning of a shape that starts at 0). A shape centred on 0
        (a push then a pull) is the alternative.
    8. 
      
  8. One curve for attack and decay. Two curves, or a hold stage, may be wanted.
    9.

        edit), and a trigger firing it (round 2's note trigger, or this timer's HOSTED target) records none. Its patch still

        refused as a trigger target?

  10. The mock arp's 'o' steps do nothing here: only TRIGGER steps fire. Arp (B539) owns the notes.
    11. 
      
  11. Several triggers on one sample (timer, arp and a click together) each fire. Should they merge into one?

ROUND 6 · FINDINGS AND OPEN QUESTIONS · B126 · round 62 M3, M4

    
    
      
  1. "The diminishing scope issue" is not named anywhere in the record. This bench builds the two shrinkings the
        record does hold. (1) Drag empties the field: B126, 2026-09-15, the human asked for "some force or process
        counteracting drag … a consistent mean magnitude". Measured on binary at drag 0.05: x's peak-to-peak is 0.19 in the
        first 10 s, 0.01 by 30 s and 0.00 by 60 s, and speed, energy and accel go the same way. (2) Small scales: round 2
        found speed and energy reading about 0.1 and 0.01 against the 3.0 clamp, which ADR-375 answered with per-preset
        scales (built here: SCALE). If the human meant something else, this is the question to ask.
    2. 
      
  2. sin and cos fix the radius, not the energy. Their range is ±1 at any distance from the centre, so a
        spiralling body still sweeps them fully (measured: binary at drag 0.3, after 35 s, x's range ≈ 10⁻⁸ while sin's is
        2.0). But they say nothing about how fast or how hard the body moves, and their rate can rise as an orbit tightens.
        The thermostat restores pace, not size. Measured on this page: binary run down by drag 0.29, then the
        thermostat on and a KICK: the mean speed came back to 0.366 (0.366 predicted), but the pair had merged, so x's
        range was 0.034 against 0.24 before, and speed's was 0.002 (a circular orbit at a held pace has a constant speed).
        sin and cos stayed at about 2.0 throughout. So for a field that has collapsed, sin and cos are the only position
        outputs with their full range left.
    3. 
      
  3. The centre is where the angle breaks down; round 6b builds both alternatives (the human: "build both, then
        decide"). Each has its own sin and cos rows, and angle about switches the tone between the three. Largest
        one-sample jump of sin or cos over 10 s, bodies 1 to 4, centre / others' COM / heading: binary 0.023 / 0.023
        / 0.014; sun 0.43 / 0.54 / 1.99; figure-eight 1.73 / 1.73 / 0.006; four-body chaos 1.75 / 1.75 / 1.99. Two
        findings. (1) Under the COM lock the others' COM gives the same angle as the centre, exactly: the lock holds
        the whole field's COM at the centre, and the others' COM then lies on the line through the body and the centre,
        on the far side, so the direction is the same. It differs only where the lock is off (sun, whose pinned sun turns
        it off), and there it is worse. (2) The heading never sees the centre, so figure-eight goes from 1.73 to
        0.006, but it jumps when a wall reflects a velocity component (sun: all six large jumps were at a wall) and when a
        body nearly stops and turns back (chaos: 26 of its 29 large jumps were away from the walls, the worst, 1.94, at a
        speed of 0.066). Not built, a third candidate: the angle about the nearest body, which is what a slingshot
        actually turns around.
    4. 
      
  4. Which sin and cos? ADR-165 A1 ratified "sin/cos observables" for Wrap: sin and cos of each
        position on the torus, so a body crossing the seam does not jump (round 62's M4 (c)). Today's ask is sin
        and cos of the angle. They are different sources, and this round builds only the angle's. Are both wanted?
        With accel and the angle's pair, a body has 8 rows (34 sources with energy and spread).

    6.

    7. 
      
  7. Auto-kick (round 6b, the human: "auto-kick a stopped field"). The reference's thermostat skips a field whose
        mean speed is at or below 10⁻⁹ (λ multiplies, so a field at rest stays at rest; round 6 measured that on this
