Is there a reason to keep two mixers?

No signal reason. The console's OSC 1, OSC 2, SUB and NOISE are Gain staging's first four stages: the same tap and the same gain. Today they're also out of step, because only the console's faders are wired: the self-check measures this below.

One real difference, which one console can show. The five faders with engine rows morph and take modulation. The other sixteen gains are global and never morph (B435), and only the sources have on, mute and solo. So an arrow in a corner colour moves with the corners, and a plain ink arrow doesn't.

What it costs. A source strip goes from 104 px to 66 px, and the other stages to about 33 px, so their readouts lose “dB”.

Things to know before you choose

One ruler in C1. You chose one scale, with each meter on its own fader's scale. That can't stretch to the whole chain: on a ±24 dB fader, a meter would show nothing under −24 dBFS. So every strip and the picture share one ruler, −66 to +26 dB. 0 dB is one straight line across the box, and each arrow travels only its own range. The cost is the tall red zone, the top 28%, because out.level reaches +26 dB.

Solo, as built here. Every stage has S except GR and the master's OUT, which is what you already hear. The sources' S work as they do today, and more than one can be on. A stage's S plays that stage's output directly, and only one can be on, because two points on one chain would double the signal. Both can apply together: solo OSC 1 and FX 2's OUT, and you hear OSC 1 through FX 2. I've assumed the tap still goes through the limiter, so a solo can't bypass the device's protection.

Bypass, as built here. BYP routes around the module, so its own gains stop applying and the wire jumps over it. Its strips read the level going past.

The width. All 21 stages need about 906 px, and the page has 940 px only with no side column. With the morph pad or Mappings open, this console scrolls sideways inside its box. See the morph and MAP question in the PR.

The scale. Today the fader and the meter have different scales, and it doesn't show because they're side by side. Out is the clear case: its 0 dB is at 70% of the fader but 92% of the meter. Put one on the other and that would be a lie, so M1 to M4 draw each meter on its own fader's scale. That means Out's red over zone is its top 30%, because its fader goes up to +26 dB. (That's M1 to M4. C1 uses the one ruler above.)

Where the room comes from. In a vertical strip the merge alone saves nothing. M0 to M3 all measure 574 × 285 px at W1 and 414 × 285 px at W2, because a strip’s width is set by its name, its peak and lamp, and M and S, not by the fader. Today’s strip fits W2 too. Only M4 changes the console’s shape. ADR-333 has you asking for a little more room here, so W1 keeps it.

What a cap covers. M1's cap sits on the bars and hides what's under it. M2 and M3 hide nothing, but their handle is smaller to grab.
