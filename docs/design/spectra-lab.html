horde spectra lab

Lab campaign 3 item 1 — *"make the engine worthwhile: find the features that give SPECTRA its own identity rather than 'the other engine'."* The shipped core was measured first, and the brief's premise turned out to be only half right. SPECTRA does not sound like SAW — it sounds DARKER than SAW (spectral centroid 562 Hz vs 2449 at matched pitch), because 12 partials at 110 Hz simply stop at 1.3 kHz. It is not competing with SAW and losing; it is playing a different, quieter game. Three measured problems shape this bench: (1) K spends 80 % of its travel doing nothing — the whole lock transition happens between 0.65 and 0.85, and below 0.45 the order parameter actually drifts DOWN; (2) the clouds are a perfectly even ramp, not a seeded draw, so `seed` cannot change the spectrum at all and every partial is identically regular; (3) the two controls SAW cannot imitate — cascade and dissolve — are healthy and buried. Cascade staggers per-partial locking; nothing in a detuned saw bank can do that. Keys A..;, Z/X octave, space drone.

Coupling — the identity controls

K taper. Measured on the shipped core: R stays at the free-run floor (~0.28) from K 0 to 0.45, drifts slightly DOWN to 0.25, then the entire lock happens between 0.65 and 0.85 — 20 % of the knob — and saturates above. "spread" remaps the knob so that transition occupies the travel. Same taper failure as ADR-059's inertia knob and the filter lab's K; fourth occurrence in this project.

cascade and dissolve are what SAW cannot do. Cascade staggers WHICH partial locks WHEN, so the spectrum re-orders itself over seconds; dissolve sets how long a coupling burst survives. Measured: dissolve 0.05 → gone immediately, 8.0 → R still 0.974 after 4.5 s, smooth throughout. These are the engine's real identity and they sit at the bottom of the panel in the plugin.

Liveliness — candidates, all default off

lock wave is cascade's mechanism made cyclic. Cascade measured as the strongest, most distinctive control in the engine, and its only limit is that it fires once and is over — a travelling band of coupling that ping-pongs along the partial series turns that gesture into a continuous animator. Which partials are locked keeps changing, which is precisely what no detuned saw bank can imitate. Watch it on the raster as a diagonal that comes back.

partial drift: SPECTRA has none at all. SAW has driftDepth/driftRate and that slow wander is most of why SAW feels alive; this gives each partial its own slow rate so the cloud centres never sit still. R → tone makes the coupling audible in the TIMBRE rather than only in the beating — positive lifts a partial as its cloud locks, negative ducks it.

Load

oscillators: —  ·  render load: —
Cost is voices × partials × cloud, so a 6-note chord at 48 partials and 7 cloud voices is 2016 running oscillators — which is what overloaded this page. Two fixes: pan is now precomputed (it is constant per partial/voice and was costing two trig calls per oscillator per sample) and the oscillator is a table lookup. Beyond that the budget thins the CLOUD on the highest partials first rather than dropping partials, because partial count is the brightness lever we want to be free to raise, and a top partial with fewer beating voices is far less audible than a missing one. The cost is shown rather than hidden — if the budget is biting, the oscillator count will sit below partials × cloud × voices.

Spectrum · the brightness gap

centroid: —  ·  top partial: —
The dim marker is SAW's measured centroid (2449 Hz at A2). SPECTRA's default lands at 562 Hz. Partial count is the lever: 12 partials at 110 Hz reach 1.3 kHz and stop. Raising partials costs CPU linearly, which is the real design question — is SPECTRA's identity "dark and evolving", or should it reach for brightness and meet SAW head-on?

Partials

tilt is the amplitude law, 1/k^tilt — at tilt = 1 that is exactly a sawtooth's spectrum, so the default IS a band-limited saw. stretch is inharmonicity (f = k·f0·√(1+Bk²)), the piano/bell axis, and it is the one axis here that a saw bank structurally cannot reach.

Clouds

Spacing is a candidate identity feature, not a shipped one. The core builds cloud offsets as `x[m] = 2m/(M−1) − 1` — a perfectly even ramp — so every partial's cloud is identically regular and the `seed` parameter cannot affect the spectrum at all (measured: 0.00 dB across seeds). SAW draws its swarm from seeded gaussian/cauchy distributions and gets its life from the irregularity. Auditioning the same choice here.
