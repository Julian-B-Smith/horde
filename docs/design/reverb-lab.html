Task #23 — the integrated reverb. Built as a full signal chain rather than a bare FDN, because the "acoustic strings section" quality the human heard from an external reverb most likely lives in the early reflections and diffusion, not in the tail length. Also tests the roadmap's open question: does the reverb get its OWN SWARM (coherent modulation, in the instrument's own idiom) or stay a static space? Audition lab, spec-in-code — NOT a reference. Keys A..;, Z/X octave, space drone.

Space

Size scales the delay-line lengths, i.e. the modelled path length between reflections. It sets mode spacing and echo spacing — the room-size cue — and deliberately does not set decay time; that is the decay control, and the loop-gain compensation keeps the two independent. Range now reaches 3.05× (was 1.55×), with the default moved to 0.20, which is exactly the old default scale of 1.05× — so nothing about the stock sound changes. Bigger is also smoother, which is the opposite of what you might expect: longer lines put the modes closer together in frequency, so measured tail roughness falls monotonically from 1.68 dB at minimum size to 1.15 dB at maximum, against a 0.20 dB floor for ideal decaying noise. Small settings are the metallic ones.
Bandpass on what is sent to the room; the dry stays untouched, and decay is unaffected (these sit outside the feedback loop, unlike damp/low cut). Cutting the lows here is the classic fix for a section that muds up when you add space.
Walks each early-reflection tap slowly and independently, smearing their fixed comb pattern over time instead of leaving it as static coloration.
—
pre trims what is sent into the room, post trims the wet return, out trims everything after the dry/wet mix. Only out can fix the crackle: measured, the dry path overflows on its own at osc volume 0.35 with four notes held, and the reverb actually lowers the peak because mix crossfades. Click the peak readout to reset the clip latch.
Note: pre and post are currently redundant — the whole reverb path is linear, so −6 dB in either place gives bit-identical output (measured max sample difference 4e−17, i.e. float rounding). They only become distinct axes once something in the loop saturates; keeping both now so that change needs no re-wiring.
Wet signal collapses to mono below this. Real rooms don't lateralise bass — the head is small against the wavelength — and hard-panned early reflections do, which headphones expose as the low end sitting on one side. 0 = off.
ER send and envelop are the two axes the space research separates: sub-50 ms early energy widens the SOURCE, >50 ms diffuse DECORRELATED energy envelops. Tail decorr is what makes the late field diffuse — at 0 the tail is mono and cannot envelop however loud it is. Their balance also sets EDT/T30: measured here 0.96 (no ER) down to 0.13 (heavy ER, low envelop), spanning the range real halls show. Mixing time 0 = stock lengths; above that the 8 delays are derived from the target rather than guessed.
Decay is set as a time and converted to per-line feedback, so changing size reshapes the room without changing how long it rings.

Early reflections

12 panned taps over the first ~80 ms. This is the hypothesis under test: that the "acoustic strings section" quality comes from the early pattern, not the tail. Pull ER send to 0 and the room should stop sounding like a place.

Modulation — the swarm question

Static FDN lines ring metallically; movement breaks it up. K = 0 is the conventional answer — eight independent drifts. As K → 1 they lock into ONE coherent breath: the instrument's own idiom applied to its room. The roadmap asks which is right; this is where you decide.

Source

Short release (default 80 ms) so the source stops quickly and everything after it is unambiguously the room. Lengthen it for pads once you have judged the space.
Hit impulse to see and hear the decay — early taps, then the diffuse tail. It is a 12 ms decaying noise burst rather than a single sample: one sample reaches the chain but cannot excite it (measured 35 dB down), so it only ever gave a tick.
