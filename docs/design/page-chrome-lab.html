B555 · The docked morph pad

      
The morph pad is on every page, in the same place: the left rail, 204 px wide, under the page tabs. The rail carries the pad, the four corners (glyph, letter, the real preset name, the edited mark, lock and revert), the QUANTUM · BLEND · STEPPED switch and a button to the Morph page, where the full editor lives. It collapses to a 56 px strip that keeps the four weights and a button to open it; it never disappears. Dragging the puck from any page morphs that page's controls live. Below 700 px of window width the rail lies down above the page as a row. A page never assumes the rail's width and never draws a second pad. On the Morph page the rail stays and the page adds the grid; on Set, whose settings are global, the rail stays too, because the pad is the instrument's one global gesture. The FX device's pages carry the same rail (ADR-254).

    

    

      
B556 · The page bank

      
Every page ends with a bank: one chip per modulator that moves at least one parameter on that page, in its kind colour (src-env, src-lfo, src-macro, src-engine), with a live activity dot and the number of routes it has on the page. The bank is computed, never kept: the mod matrix's routes against the page's own manifest keys (a depth route counts for the parameter whose depth it moves). Nothing on a page is a list of modulators. An empty bank says "No modulators on this page". Clicking a chip rings that source's mappings on the page's controls (the MAP-mode emphasis) and dims the others; click again to clear. The bank is a constant box, one line high; what does not fit folds into "+n" with the names in its tooltip. It is the Source strip's chip, filtered to the page.

    

    

      
B557 · The per-parameter morph mode

      
A morphing parameter has one of three modes, set from the "Morph mode" group of its right-click menu or from the small arrow by its name:

      
    
        
  • Follow (the default): the morph's own mode, BLEND, QUANTUM or STEPPED.
    • 
        
  • Quantum: the parameter flips at the quantum boundaries instead of blending, whatever the global mode. A continuous control shows it as a pure corner (the other corners' pips vanish, the tether goes, the needle steps); a structural one already snaps, so the item is disabled there and says why.
    • 
        
  • Exempt: the parameter leaves the morph field and holds one value (ADR-109). The pick writes the value heard now into all four corners, so nothing jumps; it is refused whole, and says so, if a locked corner would change.
    • 
      

      
The override is never colour alone: a Q (staircase) or EX (slashed ring) tag by the name; Exempt also shows no corner tint, no pips, a dashed box and "EXEMPT · held" where the corner readout was; the morph grid tags the row and moves exempt rows into their own band. It is patch state, saved with the preset (listed above), not a corner value. One pick is one undo step; re-picking the current mode makes none.

    

    

      
For you to rule

      
    
        
  • Exempt collapses the four corners to the held value (ADR-109's recorded lean, so nothing jumps either way). The alternative keeps the corners and holds a separate value, so Return to field restores the spread but jumps the sound.
    • 
        
  • Quantum on a structural parameter: disabled with a reason (here), or allowed as a no-op.
    • 
        
  • A Quantum parameter uses the field's quantum regions (the shared stand-in), not boundaries of its own. Whether a parameter may carry its own regions is the morph editor's question.
    • 
        
  • Whether a modulated parameter that is Exempt still takes its mod (here it does: Exempt is about the morph, not the modulators).
    • 
        
  • The rail's width (204 px) and whether it starts collapsed on pages that are not Main and Osc.
