Signal path — each filter drawn once, sources plugged straight in

A cable ends where the source enters. A / B: into that filter's input — in SERIAL, a source into A runs on through B (one line), and a source into B enters the line between them. A+B (PARALLEL only): into the split, so it feeds both branches, which sum at ½Σ. MIX: straight onto the mix bus Σ, past A and B, into the rack filter. DRY: around everything, straight to OUT. A filter out of the current placement is drawn dashed and passes its input through; the violet count on A and B is how many per-voice instances are running.

Filter envelope

One envelope shape (B81: a DEDICATED filter env). Per voice, every note runs its own copy (violet dots). The rack has ONE copy (violet ring), re-triggered by every note-on and released only when all keys are up — the classic mono-filter behaviour, and the audible heart of the placement difference.

Fidelity programme — seven checks × every type, each with a must-fail control

Stated as checks, not taste. Every cell is a measurement on the type's own running DSP (48 kHz, fresh instance, seeded input) against a tolerance written in the column head, and every column carries a control — a planted fault or a known-bad signal that the check MUST fail (its reading is the small second line). A control that passes would mean the check is blind; it is drawn with a red outline and counts as an error. A FAIL is a finding about the type, reported as it is — never met by loosening a tolerance. The same code runs headless in tools/labharness/filter_fidelity_check.mjs (wired into ./verify fast), which also pins today's verdicts, so a type that starts or stops passing is a deliberate, reviewed change. Hover a cell for its numbers. B290 re-cut four checks (ruled 2026-09-26, "ratified for all tolerances"): C3's level is now relative to the unfiltered source; C4 gates at the plugin's real mod tick (≈ 172 Hz) and C4b reports the fastest modulation each type survives; C5 judges a stated nominal drive (0.3, the knob default) and COST is reported beside it; C6 measures two geometries, fixed and keytracked. The last two columns are REPORTS, never gated.

Manifest — anti-aliasing, cost and maximum modulation rate, per type (B290)

One line per type: the anti-aliasing each nonlinear type carries (chosen from the evaluation below), its C5 reading at the nominal drive, its cost — time per sample of a running instance relative to SVF 12 LP, the median of seven runs, timed in this browser (the harness times the same code in node) — and its maximum viable modulation rate (C4b): the fastest per-sample modulation at which every slower rate still meets C4's bounds. That rate is the filter's manifest value (B275).

ADAA first — every anti-aliasing option for LADDER and MS-20, through the same checks

The human asked whether antiderivative anti-aliasing could reduce the need for oversampling, so each option runs through C5 (at the nominal drive; full drive is reported beside it) and through the checks ADAA's half-sample delay threatens: C1 and C2 (the small-signal filter) and C3 (self-oscillation's pitch), plus C7 for DC. ADAA ON THE WHOLE tanh breaks the zero-delay solve (C1). ADAA ON THE RESIDUAL tanh − identity keeps the linear filter exact, but its delay detunes self-oscillation (C3), and the CENTRED window that removes the delay gives back most of the suppression. The chosen option is the cheapest that passes every check. Measured live, one option per frame, after the table above.

The types — and why each is on the menu

One interface for all of them (TYPES in the DSP section): make() an instance with set(sr, P) at control rate and tick(x) per sample, H(f) its analytic small-signal response (what the curve draws and C1 checks), and claim(P) — where its cutoff and resonance knobs say the landmark is (what C2 checks). P is the same six knobs for every type: cutoff, modulation (octaves), resonance, drive, morph, mode; a type ghosts the knobs it does not read.

What the lab answers — and what is left for the human

Q1 · WHERE DOES THE FILTER SIT?Hear DEMO in each placement. Per source: each stab plucks on its own envelope while the held bass keeps its own, untouched; chords voice evenly because every note's cutoff follows that note. Rack: one envelope, re-fired by every stab, so the held bass wahs along with the melody — a pumping, glued, mono-synth character. Both: the per-voice pair does the tone, the rack slot does the motion (the plan of record — B81 per voice, B50 folds the E1 bank into the rack). The lab's lean is BOTH, but the choice of default placement for a new patch is yours.
Q2 · DOES THE SUB GET ITS OWN FILTER?The lab says no third filter. The sub's cable defaults to MIX (past A and B, into the rack) and one click sends it DRY — around everything — so "the sub stays clean" is one cable. If a sub needs shaping it can plug into A or B. A dedicated third per-voice filter would be a new parameter block to earn with a named patch the pair cannot express.
Q3 · SHARED FILTER vs PER-SOURCE MATRIXB81's pair (sources share A) collides with B23 increment 3 (the matrix wants each source separately). The lab still shares the settings and instances the state per source — exact for a linear filter (Σ filtered = filtered Σ, measured 5.55e-16 in round 1). Round 2 makes the caveat concrete: LADDER and MS-20 have a tanh in the loop, and with drive up per-source instancing is no longer the same as filtering the sum. Ruling owed.
Q4 · KEYTRACK — FROM WHICH NOTE?Per voice the answer is local: each voice tracks its own note, and the only choice is PLAYED KEY or SOURCE PITCH. On the rack there is one cutoff, so the note is a policy: LAST, LOWEST or HIGHEST, each held when keys lift. COMB + / COMB − make this sharper than before: their cutoff IS a pitch, so a rack comb keytracked to LAST re-tunes the whole mix to the melody.
Q5 · SERIAL / PARALLEL — NOW A PICTURE, AND A CHOICERound 1 applied serial/parallel per source. Drawing SERIAL as one line through two boxes makes it global (Serum's shape): a source into A runs on through B, and "A only" exists in PARALLEL (or with B set to a flat type). A source may still enter the line at B. Is the global serial line the instrument horde wants, or should serial stay per source?
Q6 · WHICH FINDINGS SHOULD BE FIXED? — B287 PHASE A DONERound 2 found 17 FAILs; the human ruled "fix all failures". Fixed (phase A): level (C6) — SVF 12 BP/HP, SVF 24 BP/HP/PEAK, the ladder and the formant now carry a stated compensation law (each type's LEVEL LAW, in the types card); the ladder's self-oscillation (C3) — its 6.7 ¢ was start-up, not tuning: k now climbs to 4·1.05⁴ over the top of the knob, so it blooms at the MS-20's rate; COMB (C2) — Lagrange, not linear, delay read; SWARM · PROPOSED (C2) — P4 range. Held, with numbers: COMB ± C6 — compensating trades the keytracked comb's level (its LEVEL LAW says by how much); SWARM · REF C2 and C6 — properties of the protected reference, pinned (PROPOSED carries P1 and P4). Phase B, under B290's ruled tolerances: aliasing (C5, now at the nominal drive 0.3) — the LADDER runs 2× oversampled (−35.9 → −64.7 dBc), the MS-20 runs centred first-order ADAA with no oversampling (−55.3 → −61.3 dBc, marginal: see the ADAA card); PHASER (C4, now at the real mod tick) — its all-pass chain is a normalised lattice, same response, and it holds C4's bounds at every rate to 22 kHz; ALL-PASS ×4 passes the tick gate either way. New, from the keytracked C6 geometry: SVF 12 and SVF 24 LP/BP/HP/PEAK, LADDER, MS-20, PHASER and SWARM · PROPOSED now FAIL C6, each REFUSED with its law room — where the two geometries disagree by more than 12 dB no resonance law can pass both (SVF LP and PEAK, the ladder, the MS-20, COMB +, PROPOSED); elsewhere only a law fitted between them could. Every scalar level law assumes where the source's energy sits, and the two geometries sit it in different places: whether C6 should demand both is the open question these numbers put to the human.
