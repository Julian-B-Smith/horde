## 1. What it is, and what it is not

The wavetable member is **a source of the swarm member's waveform**. Each member of a swarm (SW-1) keeps its
phase, its frequency, its coupling, its drift and its pan. What changes is how the member turns its phase into a
sample: it reads a band-limited cycle from a table set its oscillator shares, instead of evaluating the base wave.

- **It is not an engine.** There is one engine (GUI-1): every oscillator is a swarm whose members read wavetables.
  Nothing offers "wavetable" and "blades" (or "swarm" and "SCALPEL") as engines to choose between.
- **It replaces the blades** (ADR-387). The blade rows retire (CONTRACT §2.4): ids frozen, kept in saved state,
  hidden, inert; an old patch's blades are baked into tables at load (WT-48).
- **History.** Until ADR-387 two readings were kept open: *replace*, and *beside*, where the blades would cut a
  table member's wave as they cut a base wave. Beside would have ported every blade path to read a table's value,
  slope and span mean (about twenty call sites in the clean-room engine) at about three times the level memory; it
  is no longer a build path.
- **Why it exists** (ADR-384 context). After pass 2 of the demo bank (B645), the human concluded that blades reach
  a narrower sound space than "a mixture of FM/PM and sync on a wavetable". The demo bank used blades mostly for
  brightness, resonant peaks and formants; section 6's transformations cover those three directly.
- **Position** (ADR-337 A1). horde is discovery-oriented: rapid ideation, recombination, fluid coordinated
  morphing. It is not a precision synth, but its output is held to professional fidelity.

**In scope.** The table data model; the per-oscillator table set and its two blend laws; the member's read; the
transformations (PM, sync, warps); factory tables, recipes, capture, the user library and the blade-bake generator;
the tables and the morph; three oscillators and the PM matrix; the fidelity and CPU targets.

**Out of scope.** The GUI; the human's random wavetable generator itself (only its recipe slot, WT-46); import of
wavetable files made elsewhere; the filters and the FX rack.

## 2. Glossary

| term | meaning |
|---|---|
| **table** | 16 frames of one cycle each, stored as spectra (WT-1). |
| **frame** | One cycle of a table: 1,024 complex harmonic coefficients. Frames 0 to 15; the frame position p ∈ [0, 1] picks a point between them. |
| **corner table** | The table a morph corner holds for an oscillator (`oscN.wt.table`, WT-51). Four per oscillator. |
| **weights** | The four corner weights the table set blends at, summing to 1 (WT-10), from the morph (section 8). |
| **law** | How the corner spectra combine: **Linear** or **Spectral log** (WT-11, WT-12). |
| **level** | One band-limited rendering of a cycle holding harmonics 1 to H only (WT-4). A **level set** is every level of one cycle. |
| **published set** | What the members read: the level sets the table set last built, read-only to the voices (WT-14). |
| **rebuild** | Building a new published set at a rebuild boundary (WT-14). |
| **read rate** | How fast a member moves through the table, in cycles per second: fᵢ times every transformation's speed-up (WT-36). |
| **transformation** | Anything that changes how a member reads the table: PM, the loop, sync, sync softness, asym, bend (section 6). |
| **recipe** | What a saved patch stores for a table: how to make it again (WT-42). |
| **capture** | A new table made by freezing a blend (WT-44). |
| **c** | The brightness ceiling c = min(20 kHz, 0.45·f_int) (ADR-384 #8), f_int the internal rate (host rate × quality). |
| **detector plant**, **mutation plant** | A gate's two kinds of must-fail case: an input the meter must catch, proven engine-free at S0; and a build with one engine mechanism disabled, run at every stage close (section 9). |

## 3. The data model

| id | requirement |
|---|---|
| WT-1 | **A table is 16 frames × 1,024 harmonics.** Harmonic n of a frame is a complex coefficient cₙ = (re, im) contributing re·cos(2πnφ) + im·sin(2πnφ) at phase φ ∈ [0, 1). Harmonic 0 (DC) is always 0. Stored as float32: **128 KiB per table**. The spectra are canonical: every rendering, blend, capture and golden is derived from them (ADR-384 #1). |
| WT-2 | **Stored frames are peak-normalised.** When a table is made (a generator, a capture, the random generator), each frame is scaled so the peak of its full 1,024-harmonic cycle, rendered at 8,192 points or finer, is 1.0. A frame whose peak is below 10⁻⁶ is refused as near-silent (WT-47). This is the stored form; what the member plays is normalised by loudness (WT-16). |
| WT-3 | **Frame interpolation**, first, per corner, before the law. At p: u = 15·p, i = min(14, ⌊u⌋), f = u − i. **Linear:** the complex lerp cₙ = cₙ[i] + (cₙ[i + 1] − cₙ[i])·f. **Spectral log, OPEN** (CONTRACT §7 C6): lerping the cached log-magnitude is a geometric lerp, not \|lerp c\|, and misses QUADRANT's corner-identity tolerance by 4.7 × 10³ (H = 64) to 1.6 × 10⁴ (H = 1,024) at fractional frames (B648). **Built: the log domain,** ln\|cₙ\| = (1 − f)·ln(\|cₙ[i]\| + ε) + f·ln(\|cₙ[i + 1]\| + ε), the phase from the complex lerp. It is the same law on both axes, uses the cache, and moves a spectral peak between frames rather than crossfading two (a weighted geometric mean of two Gaussian peaks is a Gaussian at the weighted mean position). The alternative, exact, costs a log and a hypot per harmonic per corner per rebuild. So the two laws hear different in-between frames of one table, by design; WT-F10 tests each law against its own rule. |
| WT-4 | **Levels.** A level holds harmonics 1 to H. For a level serving read rates up to r_hi: **H·r_hi ≤ c** (ADR-384 #8). Levels are spaced by a ratio ρ in read rate, H = ⌊2^(k·log₂ρ)⌋ for k = 0, 1, …, up to H = 1,024. **Built: ρ = 2, one level per octave** (ADR-384 #1), ρ one named constant so S3 can measure and the human hear ρ = 2^½ and 2^¼ beside it (OPEN, CONTRACT §7 C1). Engine-free: at one level per octave the top harmonic falls to c/2 (about 9.9 kHz at 44.1 kHz) at the bottom of each level's range. |
| WT-5 | **Pitch crossfade** (ADR-384 #8). In the upper third of each level's range in log read rate (PROPOSED), a member plays the level and its darker neighbour, mixed per sample by C.0's raised cosine of the position, so a glide never steps. The band limit stays exact, but the top harmonics fade early (B648). Engine-free, the loss in the 16 to 20 kHz band is in WT-F2's row; half-octave levels halve it for about twice the memory (CONTRACT §1.5). |
| WT-6 | **Level sizes and the read.** A level of H harmonics is N samples of one cycle, N ≥ 2H, with guard samples so no read wraps mid-kernel. The read and N are the builder's, judged by WT-F1. Engine-free, on a 1/n-saw table by QS-1's method at 44.1 kHz (C1, C2, C4, C6, C8): linear at N = 2048 reads 54, 64, 83, 100, 116 dB (fails QS-1 below C6); 4-point cubic at N = max(2048, 32·H) reads 118, 115, 109, 138, 140 dB as Catmull-Rom; **the cubic fallback is 4-point Lagrange** (ADR-390, P7), which S0 reads at ≥ 129.7 dB at every pitch at 32·H, against Catmull-Rom's 109.4 at C4 (S0-RESULTS §5); **6-point Lagrange at N = min(8192, max(2048, 16·H)) reads the 140 dB cap at C1, C2 and C4 (144 KiB per one-per-octave level set, the largest level 32 KiB): built**; 8-point capped at 4,096 reads 128 dB at C1 and the cap at C2 and C4 (112 KiB, largest 16 KiB), the fallback if the timed runs show cache misses. The H = 1,024 level (below about 19.5 Hz) has 8 samples per period: reported, not judged. |
| WT-7 | **Memory and cache.** Large levels cost memory and cache misses, and instruction counts do not see cache misses (B648): the timed runs judge them (WT-P3). The 2048-sample cycle is the exact time-domain image of one frame, the form of export, previews and the goldens' time-domain check; it is not required to be the form the member reads. |
| WT-8 | **Memory is stated** (PF-7) by the manifest at the build's ρ, N and Linear strategy (WT-23), reported every stage. **PROPOSED cap: 24 MiB per oscillator's table set**: 48 MiB for two, 72 MiB for three (WT-60). CONTRACT §1.5 gives the figures. |
| WT-9 | **Every level exists before it is read.** A table set builds **every** level of a published set at each rebuild, not only those a sounding pitch uses, so a new note never meets a missing level. (A builder who builds only the levels in use must show the result is bit-identical at every pitch, including a note started mid-interval; otherwise this rule holds.) |

## 4. The table set (per oscillator, per instance)

One table set per oscillator per plug-in instance, shared by every voice and member of that oscillator. Its cost
is flat in the voice count (QUADRANT's central decision, kept).

| id | requirement |
|---|---|
| WT-10 | **Inputs.** Four corner tables (slots A to D) and four weights w = [w_A, w_B, w_C, w_D], each in [0, 1], summing to 1, from the morph (WT-53); the law (`wt.law`); the frame (section 5). Blending four different tables at any weights is the 1.0 design (ADR-377 A3 / ADR-385). |
| WT-11 | **Linear.** cₙ = Σ_c w_c·cₙ[c], on the frame-interpolated coefficients (WT-3). It equals a bilinear crossfade of the four corners' time-domain cycles; harmonics in opposed phase cancel and the centre can thin (WT-16 restores its loudness). |
| WT-12 | **Spectral log.** \|cₙ\| = max(0, exp(Σ_c w_c·Lₙ[c]) − ε), Lₙ[c] = ln(\|cₙ[c]\| + ε) interpolated by WT-3's log rule, ε = 10⁻⁴. A corner with weight 0 contributes nothing. The phase is arg(Σ_c w_c·cₙ[c]); where that sum is below 10⁻⁶ in magnitude, the phase of the heaviest corner, the lowest (A, B, C, D order) on a tie. The cache is derived data, never state. A fixed phase is the alternative to hear at S2 (QUADRANT's open question). |
| WT-13 | **Order.** Per corner, frame interpolation; then the law; then levels and loudness (WT-16); then publication. |
| WT-14 | **Rebuild on change, at control rate** (ADR-384 #7). A boundary falls every **R = 4 control intervals** (PA-3: 116 samples at 44.1 kHz, 128 at 48 kHz; ADR-384 #7's 128 samples stated in time, SW-14; PROPOSED). At a boundary the table set rebuilds only if a corner table, a weight, the law or (under Spectral log) the instance frame changed. A static patch rebuilds **zero** times, counted (IF-9). Boundaries are counted on the engine's sample clock (PA-3), so the result is independent of block size (DT-2). The rebuild runs on the audio thread with bounded work (RT-2) and no allocation (RT-1). |
| WT-15 | **The crossfade between published sets lasts R** (ADR-384 #7), by C.0's equal-gain raised cosine. A set that arrives during a crossfade replaces the pending one and starts when the running one ends; at most two sets are heard, three exist. Loudness changes (WT-16) ride it. **This crossfade is also the transition for a change of the law, of a corner's table, and of a Quantum flip of the tables** (WT-38): the set is per instance and shared by the voice's PA-5 copy and its live swarm, so PA-5 cannot crossfade it. **The scheme** (CONTRACT §7 C7, answered by ADR-390; B648: under motion a crossfade always runs, so every member read touches both sets, 4 table reads with the pitch crossfade). Three schemes: **(i)** each member reads both sets (exact); **(ii)** a fused read of both sets at one index, sharing the interpolation coefficients; **(iii)** the table set writes (1 − g)·old + g·new once per control interval and members read one set, a staircase of 4 steps across R. **Built: (iii), one interpolated set per interval** (ADR-390; the timed run read 4.80 % of a core against (i)'s 6.49 and (ii)'s 5.19), still subject to WT-F4's moving-phase probe (WT-F4 can fail it). |
| WT-16 | **Loudness, level-matched to the base saw, with a safety peak limit** (ADR-384 #10). No user toggle. (1) **Loudness:** the blended spectrum is scaled so its **K-weighted** RMS (ITU-R BS.1770's K-weighting magnitude at each harmonic's frequency n × 130.81 Hz, C3, harmonics under 20 kHz there) equals that of the band-limited ±1 saw at C3, which is the base saw a table member replaces (`waves.h`'s saw). The weights are fixed, so the gain does not depend on the played pitch. Engine-free, on the factory set's frames 0, 5, 10 and 15 (32 frames, one member): this leaves a loudness spread of **0.00 dB at C3, 1.58 dB at C2 and 2.46 dB at C5**; plain Parseval RMS would leave 2.42 dB at C3 (−0.66 to +1.76, Vowel the loudest). (2) **Peak limit 2.6** (PROPOSED): if the scaled top level's peak exceeds it, the gain is reduced to meet it, counted. Engine-free, at this loudness the factory frames peak at up to 2.27 (crest factors 1.18 to 4.36, median 1.93), so the limiter is a safety, not the common path; a peak above 1.0 is allowed, as the engine guarantees none (OU-1, QS-8). (3) **Gain cap +24 dB** (PROPOSED), so a cancelling blend fades instead of amplifying rounding noise, counted. Under Linear's read-time strategy (WT-23) the gain comes exactly from the corners' K-weighted Gram matrix and the peak limit from the bound Σ_c w_c·peak_c, which can only over-limit. |
| WT-17 | **Determinism.** Same state and events give the same output sample for sample on one build (DT-1), at any block split (DT-2). The table set reads no clock (DT-5) and draws no random numbers. After `reset()` it is as a fresh prepare with the same state (DT-6). |
| WT-18 | **Tables arrive as events, built in full first.** A state load and a `set_table` call build every table they name, its spectra and its level sets, **on the calling (main) thread, completely, before they return**; never on the audio thread and never on a worker the audio thread waits for. A table assigned while the engine plays reaches it as a **sample-timed event** (the first sample of the shell's next processing call, recorded in the event stream), so a replay of the same events applies it at the same sample. After a load, the first published set exists before the first sample renders: no silent first R samples. So no table becomes audible at a time that depends on a thread's speed (WT-F5's load case). |
| WT-19 | **The base/table switch is a test switch, gone in 1.0** (ADR-388 #4c). Through S8 the engine's state holds, per oscillator, `member`: `base` or `table` (not a manifest row, not a host parameter, never shown on a page). Its one job is the oracle: with every `member` at `base` the engine renders the blade patches bit-identically to the pinned clean-room commit (WT-P4). The 1.0 round migrates every state to `table` (old patches through the blade bake, WT-48) and removes `base`, so 1.0 has no second path. A change while notes sound crossfades by PA-5. |

## 5. The member

| id | requirement |
|---|---|
| WT-20 | **Phase from the swarm, unchanged.** θᵢ, fᵢ, the start phase (SW-10), the onset wait (SW-12), drift, coupling and inertia are the swarm's (SW-1 to SW-17). The table is read at **φ = θᵢ**. In the clean-room engine the base wave is `wave(base, θᵢ + ½)` (`blade/waves.h`), so its saw is 2θᵢ − 1, rising from −1 at θᵢ = 0, and its sine is −sin 2πθᵢ. **The factory Sine → saw table has the same polarity** (its coefficients are the negated bench ones, WT-40), so a patch moved from the base wave to that table keeps its polarity at frames 0 and 15. |
| WT-21 | **Reads.** With no crossfade running and the read rate inside one level, a member makes one interpolated read per internal sample of each cycle it plays: one under Spectral log; under Linear two (adjacent frames) with a pre-blend or eight (four corners × two frames) with a read-time blend (WT-23). WT-15's and WT-5's crossfades each double that while they run. Counted (IF-9). |
| WT-22 | **The frame.** **Under Linear it is per voice** (ADR-384 #6): each voice reads at its resolved `wt.frame` plus its per-voice offsets (PA-2). **Frame spread:** member i reads at pᵢ = clamp(p + `wt.frameSpread`·sᵢ, 0, 1), sᵢ its position from the oscillator's spread law (C.10, BL-13; a single member has sᵢ = 0). **Under Spectral log it is global** (ADR-384 #6): the table set rebuilds at the frame of **the newest held voice** (MS-46's rule; with none held, the last such value), offsets included; frame spread is inert. **OPEN** (CONTRACT §7 I6): the lab's model, all 16 frames pre-blended so the frame is per voice under both laws, if S4's timed run affords it. |
| WT-23 | **Linear's strategy, and its cost.** Two strategies meet WT-11; the builder picks by measurement against WT-BUDGET and WT-8 (CONTRACT §7 C9). **(a) read-time blend:** level sets for every corner frame (4 × 16, plus staging, WT-18), built when a table arrives; each member reads 4 corners × 2 frames and sums; no rebuild. **(b) pre-blend:** at each rebuild, 16 blended frames B_f = Σ_c w_c·T_{c,f}, then 2 reads per member-sample; built from the blended spectra by inverse FFT (16 per rebuild while the weights move), or from (a)'s corner level sets by weighted sums (then (a)'s memory too). Neither may change the sound beyond WT-F10's tolerance. |
| WT-24 | **What the member hands on, and the restart correction** (ADR-389). The member's read, times SW-13's band-edge fade, enters the swarm before its PA-6 fade, entry gain, density scale and pan: where the clean-room engine's y⁰ sits (CONTRACT §1.1). **A sync restart is corrected only when it has a step.** Its value step is **Δ = g·(y(φ⁺) − y(φ⁻))**, with **g = 1 at `wt.syncSoft` = 0** (the default hard cut) **and g = 1 − depth otherwise** (WT-33, as the lab does), y read at the actual read phases on either side of the restart (after the warp and every phase push, WT-34, WT-61, WT-70); its slope corner likewise. **Crossing an integer ratio** switches nothing in the correction's size: Δ and the corner go to 0 continuously as frac r → 0, and at an exact integer (1 included) they are exactly 0 and the correction is skipped as a branch, bit-identical to computing it (B661). **The smooth part's delay (CONTRACT §7 J2, answered by ADR-390).** ED-1's step correction delays each step by the table's τ, about 2.4 samples, while a curved smooth part between steps gets no delay unless something delays it: that mismatch is B-51, which round 42 fixed by filter E for DT-3 (ADR-289), not for QS-1. A synced table read is exactly a curved smooth part with steps, so a correction without a matching delay of the smooth part reintroduces B-51 at every ratio that is not whole; and before round 42, ED-6 left curved corners uncorrected for the same reason (a corner residual's tail ends only when the smooth part is delayed by τ). **The restart correction is the delayed read: the table read itself is delayed by τ on every path at every ratio** (ADR-390, P1; read at θᵢ − τ·fᵢ/f_int, exact for a table; not a filter; nothing switches; ADR-389 holds), so the step and corner residuals meet a smooth part delayed as ED-1's design assumes. **Proven engine-free by S0 item 1** (`docs/wavetable/S0-RESULTS.md` §1): DT-3 within 0.17 dB on every case (worst 0.166, bound 0.5), as band-limited as E within 0.4 dB by QS-1's meter, B-51's control failing by 14.6 to 40.4 dB, and r = 1 and 2 exact; WT-F42 judges the build. The alternative, E with a PA-5 crossfade at integer crossings, reintroduces the switching ADR-389 removed, combs while it fades and costs a 33- to 65-tap filter per member-sample. |
| WT-25 | **DC.** Tables have no DC. The cycle means the transformations make are removed so QS-6's bound holds (WT-F8): **sync** exactly, from the table's antiderivative Y (harmonic n ÷ 2πn·i), mean = (Y(frac r) − Y(0))/r; the restart corrections add no mean term under the delayed read (ADR-390, P4: S0 measured their mean at −84 dB re the output's RMS or lower), **provided τ equals the read table's own −Ā within 10⁻³ sample**, Ā the step residual's area (S0-RESULTS §1, finding 1c); **the warp, and the softness taper**, numerically, by the midpoint rule over one member cycle with J ≥ 4·H points, recomputed at C.16's cadence and followed through C.16's 7.56 ms one-pole. C.16's 8 Hz blocker is the fallback for the loop (WT-31). |
| WT-26 | **Real-time safety.** RT-1 to RT-4 hold. Everything is allocated at prepare for the maxima (64 voices, 9 members, ADR-381; K oscillators, WT-60). The table handoff never waits or locks on the audio thread (CONTRACT §1.6). |

## 6. Transformations

**The read chain**, per member per sample (each stage off at its neutral value): θᵢ → **sync** (WT-32) → **the
warp** (WT-34) → **phase push** (the PM matrix, WT-61; the internal modulator, WT-70, pending the lab; the loop, WT-31) → the read → **softness taper** (WT-33) → the member's output.
Sync sets how many table cycles fit the member's cycle, the warp reshapes time inside each, PM pushes the result.

| id | requirement |
|---|---|
| WT-30 | **PM between oscillators: the matrix.** Superseded by section 6a (ADR-385 A1): every oscillator's read phase takes PM from every other oscillator, and from itself through WT-31's feedback. |
| WT-31 | **The loop: cross-mod and feedback, reused.** For a table member, `crossMod`, `feedback` and `loopDrive` act by C.14's law with xinᵢ added to the read phase: feedback is self-PM, cross-mod rings the members through one another. No new row. **`feedback` is the PM matrix's diagonal** (WT-61): an oscillator's PM from itself. **This amends three things** (CONTRACT §8): C.14 (xin today offsets only Sync, FM and Ring carriers), C.16 (its blocker engages while the loop is live, which must include a table member), and PA-8's live conditions for the three rows (today each needs a blade on in Sync, FM or Ring). ## 6a. Three oscillators and the PM matrix (ADR-385 A1)

| id | requirement |
|---|---|
| WT-60 | **The oscillator count K is a build parameter from S1**, 2 or 3, fixed at compile time. The table sets, the harness, the PM buffers, the counters and the observation view are sized by it. **Every gate runs at K = 3**, and so does the clean-room seam from S7 (ADR-388 #4a). A K = 3 render with osc 3 off and every matrix entry naming it at 0 is **bit-identical** to the same patch at K = 2. Osc 3 starts off in a new patch (PROPOSED, CONTRACT §7 H3). |
| WT-61 | **The matrix.** A K × K depth matrix D: **row i is the oscillator modulated, column j the modulator.** Each off-diagonal entry is a manifest row `oscI.wt.pmJ` (CONTRACT §2.1): an index 0 to 10, `pow` law, Morphable (a corner value: it blends by MO-9 and flips under Quantum), audio-rate (PA-4, as E1 made the single PM row), per-voice (PA-2). Member k of osc i has its read phase pushed by Σ_{j ≠ i} (D[i][j]/2π)·m_{j,k}, BL-5's phase-FM form. **The diagonal D[i][i] is osc i's `feedback`** (C.14, WT-31): no new row, shown in the diagonal of the matrix's page. |
| WT-62 | **The modulator, for every column.** m_{j,k} is osc j's member (k mod U_j) output after its own PA-6 fade and entry gain, times **osc j's governing envelope and osc j's on-fade** (the voice's per-source fade, `osc_fade_` in the clean-room voice), not its density scale, level, pan or gate. So a modulator can be silent (level −∞) and still modulate, its envelope shapes the index, and when it switches off its PM fades with it and never drops in one sample. During osc j's PA-5 crossfade, m is the crossfade's mix (by the same raised cosine) of the copy's and the live swarm's member outputs; during a change of osc j's unison, the old and new member mappings are mixed over PA-6's 5 ms. An oscillator that is off modulates nothing (MO-26, RC-6.3). |
| WT-63 | **Render order and edges.** Within a voice the oscillators render in the fixed order **osc K, …, osc 2, osc 1**, so the classic operator stack 3 → 2 → 1, with osc 1 the carrier heard, has no delay anywhere. An entry D[i][j] with **j > i** (the modulator renders earlier) is a **forward edge**: it reads osc j's output at the same internal sample. An entry with **j < i** (1 → 2, 1 → 3, 2 → 3) is a **back-edge**: it reads osc j's output **one internal sample late**, averaged over the two latest samples, ½·(y_j[n − 1] + y_j[n − 2]), as C.14's feedback is (the averaging damps the period-2 ringing a one-sample loop makes; the law is CONTRACT §7 H2). The diagonal is C.14's, one host sample late. The order is a constant, never chosen by the patch. |
| WT-64 | **Two render modes, one result.** **Segment mode** (the clean-room voice's today): each oscillator over a whole segment in WT-63's order, forward modulators kept in a buffer (WT-66). **Interleaved mode:** all K oscillators advanced one internal sample at a time in WT-63's order. A back-edge needs the interleaved mode (its modulator's previous sample does not exist yet in segment mode); a voice uses it for a segment in which any back-edge depth, resolved with its offsets, is above 0 or still gliding to 0, and segment mode otherwise. **A patch whose back-edges are all 0 renders bit-identically in both modes** (gated): the per-member arithmetic is the same, only the loop order differs. **Cost:** interleaving keeps K swarms' member states in use at once (in the clean-room engine a member is about 2.4 KiB, mostly its `pending` ring and E history: 3 × 9 × 2.4 ≈ 65 KiB per voice) and repeats per-sample work the segment loop amortises; PROPOSED estimate +10 to 25 % on the oscillators' instruction count while a back-edge is live, unverified, measured at S5b as cell 5 against cell 4 (WT-P2). |
| WT-65 | **Stability clamp.** Back-edges and the diagonal close loops. Each back-edge depth, resolved with its offsets, is clamped to **I_back = 2.5** (ADR-390, P5: one grid step under 2.75, the largest equal index at which S0's engine-free stability map, B521's method, shows a 1 ↔ 2 loop on Sine → saw frame 0 still contracting from C1 to C6; the map stops at C6), and each engagement is counted (IF-9) and shown on the page, never silent. The diagonal keeps C.14's `loopDrive` law and WT-31's map. Forward edges close no loop and are not clamped: **a forward depth of 5 to 10 runs the loop away at a back-edge index of 0.75 to 1.0, whatever the clamp** (S0-RESULTS §3 B). Whatever the depths, the output is bounded: a table read is bounded, so a runaway loop is noise, not a blow-up (WT-F12 fuzzes it). |
| WT-66 | **The PM buffers.** In segment mode each forward modulator keeps its member outputs for the segment: at most K − 1 oscillators × 9 members × the segment at the internal rate, **144 KiB at High for K = 3** (2 × 9 × 1,024 doubles; 72 KiB at Standard), one shared scratch since voices render one at a time. Interleaved mode keeps each member's two latest outputs instead, and needs a work buffer per oscillator for High's decimation at the segment's end (K × 2 × 1,024 doubles, 48 KiB). |
| WT-67 | **Aliasing.** The matrix aliases by design at Standard (WT-37's PM row; QS-3 reports it); a carrier's level is chosen without PM (WT-36). |
| WT-68 | **Cost per live entry** is one multiply-add per carrier member-sample: the modulator values exist already. The matrix's cost is the third oscillator itself and, with a back-edge, the interleaving (WT-64). |
| WT-69 | **The 1.0 manifest at three oscillators** (ADR-387, ADR-388 #4b). Osc 3's block is **0x8000**, with osc 1's offsets and no blade blocks and none of the six blade-only rows; `mix.osc3` is voice offset 0x0013; every matrix row is live (`osc1.wt.pm3` and `osc2.wt.pm3` included). Ids and their verification: CONTRACT §2.1, §2.2, §10. The K = 2 build (WT-60) is a test build only. |
| WT-70 | **The internal modulator, PROPOSED, pending the lab** (B652: not final until the human rules the lab). One modulator per member of each oscillator: a **shape** (sine, triangle, saw, reverse saw, square, smooth noise, S&H noise, the blades' `modShape` list); a **rate**, as a ratio of the member's frequency or in Hz; a **type**, phase (pushes the read phase by (I/2π)·m) or pitch (bends the read rate by (1 + I/10)^m, BL-5's law, accumulated into a read-phase offset so θᵢ and the swarm are untouched); a **depth**, index 0 to 10; and **per-member decorrelation**: rate and phase spread across the members, and an independent noise stream per member, as SCALPEL's jitter presets do. Noise draws come from mulberry32 streams keyed by note, member and purpose (`wt/mod/…`, DT-4). Its rows are proposed, with ids held, in CONTRACT §2.1. |
