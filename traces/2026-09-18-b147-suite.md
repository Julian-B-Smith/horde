# b147-suite — six standalone fidelity checks for the SWARM SAW engine

- **Queue item:** B147 layer 2 (the suite; layer 1 was the audit at
  `docs/audits/2026-09-18-saw-engine-audit.md`, branch `lead-records-38`, PR #628)
- **Why:** the audit's §3 enumerated six oracle gaps and measured a threshold
  for each. Layer 2 turns those measurements into checks, so the numbers stop
  being a document and start being falsifiable. Nothing is wired into
  `./verify` — that is the human's ruling (ADR-171 is the route); all six are
  standalone targets on the `polarity_check` pattern.
- **Evidence consulted:** `docs/audits/2026-09-18-saw-engine-audit.md` §§1.4,
  1.5, 3.1–3.8 and §5; `src/swarm_core.h` (`:324` Voice is public, `:1631`
  the 0.08 smoother, `:1805-1813` the spring, `:1815-1819` the output pole,
  `:1837` apZ, `:854-879` the voice cull); `tools/samplerate_check.cpp` and
  `tools/subdiv_check.cpp` (the two gates these cover the blind spots of —
  neither touched); `tools/measure_alias.cpp`, `tools/measure_cpu.cpp`,
  `tools/robustness_matrix.cpp:155`; `tools/polarity_check.cpp` (the
  standalone-and-unwired idiom); LIBRARY L0016 / L0024 / L0032 / L0033 /
  L0036 / L0051 / L0053.
- **Alternatives rejected:**
  - *A third FFT for the swarm aliasing rows.* The audit's reduction note
    forbids it by name. `alias_check` is `tools/measure_alias.cpp` built a
    second time with `-DALIAS_JUDGE=1`, so the judgement sits beside the
    calibrated FFT it judges and `measure_alias` keeps its "print, never
    judge" contract byte for byte (verified: same table, exit 0). Same shape
    for `cpu_check` / `measure_cpu` with `-DCPU_JUDGE=1`.
  - *A second stopwatch for CPU.* Same reason.
  - *Editing `samplerate_check` or `subdiv_check`.* Both are gates and out of
    scope; `sr_check` and `blocksize_check` sit beside them instead. The cost
    is one copied `attackSeconds` probe, which is cheaper than editing a gate.
  - *A rep-spread guard on `cpu_check`.* Built, measured, deleted: its good
    and bad bands overlap (1.01–1.33x on a machine matching the audit to 5 %,
    1.63x under a parallel build), so no threshold between them is defensible.
    Recorded in the file rather than silently removed.
  - *Computing each bar as `measured + margin` at runtime.* That bar passes by
    construction and can never fail. Every bar is a literal derived from the
    audit's recorded figure.

### sr_check — sample-rate independence in SECONDS (44.1 / 48 / 88.2 / 96 k)

| quantity | audit | measured | bar | margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-step to 90 %, drift vs 44.1 k | 54.9 % | 54.876 % | 65.9 % | audit + 20 % |
| onset-lock t(R peak), dissolve 0.30 | 5.56 % | 5.739 % | 6.67 % | audit + 20 % |
| inertia steady-state R, spread | 15.03 % | **2.103 %** | 18.04 % | audit + 20 % |
| output pole at 10 kHz | 0.40 dB | 0.400 dB | 0.48 dB | audit + 20 % |

Per-rate values, 44.1 / 48 / 88.2 / 96 k: K step 0.00992 / 0.00909 / 0.00503 /
0.00448 s; lock peak (dissolve 0.30) 0.04459 / 0.04427 / 0.04223 / 0.04203 s;
inertia R 0.28006 / 0.28162 / 0.28555 / 0.27967; pole −0.623 / −0.687 / −0.998 /
−1.023 dB. Lock peaks at dissolve 0.02 / 0.05 / 0.30 read 44.18 / 45.67 /
44.59 ms at 44.1 k against the audit's 44.4 / 45.9 / 44.9.

Margin = 20 % relative: the two probes disagree by up to 3.2 % on the same
quantity (0.2 ms grid + parabolic refinement here vs the audit's 0.5 ms grid),
so the margin is ~6x the observed probe-to-probe difference.

**The inertia row does not confirm the audit's 15.03 %, and the reason is a
control.** R in that patch wanders: over the audit's 10–12 s window it reads
0.25485 at 44.1 k and 0.29398 over 20–30 s at the SAME rate — 15.3 % with the
sample rate held fixed. Averaged over 10–120 s the within-rate wander falls to
3.61 % and the cross-rate spread reads 2.103 %, i.e. BELOW its own control. The
honest current reading is "no rate dependence of this quantity is resolvable
above its wander". The 15.03 % survives as the bar it must not grow back to.

## Open questions

1. **`denormal_check` is RED and I did not make it green.** The ADR-074 mode-D
   allpass (`swarm_core.h:1837`, `apZ`) decays into the subnormal float range
   with a silent input: 334 subnormal output samples over a 30 s tail at
   width 1.3 / superMode 3, none at all with the allpass off. That is the audit's
   own §1.5 prediction landing, and it is the first measurement of it. It costs
   nothing on this M3 (the audit's control says so) and is a scheduler hazard on
   the x86 min-spec in ACCEPTANCE L0-6. Fixing it is a `src/` change (a flush, or
   a DC offset in the allpass state) and out of this brief's scope. Ruling needed.
2. **`apZ` and `hb[]` cannot be read** — private to `SwarmCore`, no accessor, no
   debug export. `denormal_check` reaches them only through the output. Closing
   that needs one accessor in `src/`.
3. **The audit's 15.03 % inertia figure should probably be retired or restated**
   in ACCEPTANCE (see the sr_check section). It is a 2 s window on a wandering
   quantity, and its own same-rate control reads 15.3 %.
4. **`seed` is inert on the default patch** — 0.0 difference between seed 1234
   and 999999 at dist 0/1/4 with panScatter 0 and scatter 0; 0.798 at dist 2,
   0.682 at dist 3, 0.062 with panScatter 0.5, 0.738 with scatter 1. This is
   by construction (no randomised feature engaged draws `grng`) and is NOT the
   audit's gap #3, which is about the unseeded ensemble-timing stream. Recorded
   in `blocksize_check` as a plant that did not fire (L0033) rather than retried
   away.
5. **Wiring any of these into `./verify` is untouched and unproposed here** —
   ADR-171 is the route, and `stability_check` at 73 s is a `full` candidate at
   best.
