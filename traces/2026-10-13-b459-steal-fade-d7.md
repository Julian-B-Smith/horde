# b459-steal-fade-d7 — a voice taken while it sounds fades, and the new note starts when the fade ends (D7 `stealFade`, OFF by default)

> Round 1 (below, as written) stopped with D7 unledgered and nothing pushed. **Round 2, further down, closes that:** the ledger check was extended (ROADMAP B465) and D7 is in the ledger. **Round 3, at the end, is the rework after an independent critic's read:** it changes the law in five places, the check in three, and corrects two of round 1's statements (points 5 and 11 of "The law as built", marked there). Where an earlier round disagrees with a later one, the later one is the present state. **Round 4, at the end, replaces round 3's release rule** (a stolen note is now late by its wait in every respect) and records the merge of `main`.

- **Queue item:** ROADMAP B459, under ADR-216 (the human's ruling of 2026-10-12, items 2 and 3, and the lead's details under it). Built by an implementer dispatch from the lead, on top of `h2-frame-hold-d6` (draft PR #1072, B461).
- **Why:** with the pool full a poly note-on takes a voice that is still sounding and restarts it as a fresh voice: the envelope keeps its level while the phases, filters and DC state reset, so the output jumps in one sample. D7 fades the taken voice over 0.005 s and starts the note from silence at the sample the fade ends. It is a flag in the C++ engine and in the composed JS, default OFF, so no existing digest moves (ADR-187 A1).

## The take paths (`h2/engine/engine.h`, `noteOn`)

Every path ends at ONE call, `startVoice(*v, note, freq, vel, true, true)`, and the take is decided on the line before it: `v->wait || (stealFade && v->active && v->env >= 1e-3)`. The picks that can reach it with a sounding voice:

1. **Tier 2, no cap:** `tierPick(P, kSkipNone)`, its second loop: the quietest releasing tail.
2. **Tier 3, no cap:** the loop after the steal policy, `!busy`: the oldest voice when every slot is gated.
3. **At a voice cap (`busy`):** `tierPick(P, kSkipInactive)`: the quietest sounding tail, a culled one included.
4. **At a full cap, `capPolicy` 2:** the same loop, among sounding voices: the oldest held.
5. **At a full cap, `capPolicy` 1 with no free slot:** `h && f` fails and it falls through to that loop.

Not takes: tier 1 (a slot under 1e-3, sounding or not, restarts at once, as before); `capPolicy` 1 with a free slot (the new note starts on the free slot; the stolen voice goes to the cull's fade, as before); `capPolicy` 0 (refused); `monoOn`; and the parity check's fault 6 (the blade oracle's own voice law, a must-fail control), which is untouched. The JS has the same single site.

## The law as built, and where the ruling left a choice

The ruling's five rules are built as written. Where the code needed more, each is the lead's to overrule:

1. **The fade is counted in whole samples:** `round(0.005 * sr)`, 240 at 48 kHz and 221 at 44.1 kHz. After k of its N samples the gain is (N - k) / N: the cull's per-sample scaling of `env` (and of each member's envelope on a per-partial voice), with an integer count in place of the cull's running float, so the end is an exact sample.
2. **The old sound is released at the take** (`gate` off, stage 4). The voice is then a releasing tail under a ramp, the waiting note alone says who holds it, and a note-off for the old key finds nothing at all.
3. **The waiting voice takes the note's age at the take and keeps it when the note starts.** Without the first, tier 3 would pick the same oldest voice for every note of a chord and each would replace the last. Without the second, a note that waited would look newer than one struck after it. So which voice is picked is what the present law picks.
4. **Notes whose fades end on the same sample start in the order they were struck** (by that age), so the shared random stream is drawn in strike order.
5. **The fade runs its whole length even if the old voice's own release ends first** (R 5 ms: the row `a tail under the threshold, and one that dies inside its fade`). The lateness is then always the fade's length. *(Corrected in round 3: it was not, for a culled tail that is taken. See point 6 and round 3, M2.)*
6. **A voice already fading under the cull:** the steal's ramp takes over from the cull's present gain over `ceil(cullG * 0.008 * sr)` samples, the cull's remaining length, and the voice stops being a culled tail (it is the note's). Not restarted, not lengthened. *(Round 3, M2: such a note waited up to 8 ms, 257 to 369 samples in the critic's probe. The take-over is now capped at the steal's own 240.)*
7. **A fade outlives its wait.** A panic (or a mode change, or the steal policy below) cancels the note; the ramp runs out and frees the slot. A voice in such a fade is not live, like a culled one, and the cull skips it. If a later note takes it while it still sounds, the note attaches to the running fade.
8. **`capPolicy` 1 can meet a note that still waits** (the ruling does not say). A waiting voice counts as held, so it can be the oldest held; stealing it cancels the waiting note, and the new note takes the free slot. Its fade is already the fade the steal would have given it. Row: `capPolicy 1 cancels a waiting note`.
9. **A voice a note waits on takes a newer note whatever its level and whatever the flag** (the replace rule), so a wait is never left standing when the flag is switched off mid-phrase.
10. **A mode change (`polyMode`) cancels every waiting note,** as a panic does: mono has one voice and its own law.
11. **Keep-phase** reads the newest sounding swarm at the cut, as at the end of any host render. A waiting note is the newest by age, so with `keepPhase` on it continues its own slot's old phases. *(WRONG AS BUILT IN ROUND 1, found by the critic: at the cut the faded voice had just been set inactive, so the snapshot skipped it. With another voice sounding the note took THAT voice's phases; with none, it read the snapshot left at the host's last block boundary, and the output depended on the host block size. Fixed in round 3, B1; the sentence is true of the round-3 build.)*

The render is cut at EVERY steal-fade end, waited on or not, and each part runs the whole of the old `render()` body (the cull's pass, gravity's segments, the keep-phase snapshot). That is what a host note-on delivered at that sample gives, and it keeps the per-sample path to exactly the fade's samples at any host block size. *(The block-size claim was false with `keepPhase` on and the pool taken whole until round 3, B1.)*

## What the check can do now that it could not (`tools/labharness/divergence_ledger_check.mjs`)

1. **A scenario may carry a note script.** A scope scenario is a preset name, or `{preset, over, sr}`, or that with `script`: a list of `"on <note>..."`, `"off <note>..."` and `"render <samples>"` events (velocity 0.9, 440 * 2^((note - 69) / 12) Hz, 128-sample blocks), fingerprinted the same way (SHA-256 of the float32 L then R). L1 checks a script's shape with the grammar the render uses.
2. **L8, new: every engine divergence flag is a built entry's.** The flags are DERIVED, not listed in the check: the lab's own push list, `const AA189 = [...]` in `docs/design/scalpel-interface-lab.html`, read from the lab's text (the list `oracleParams()` walks to send each divergence toggle to the engine: a flag missing from it has a dead toggle), and the flags of the engine's registered SwarmSynth patches (`Composed.swarmPatches`, L7's source). Each must be an engine key and a built entry's flag. A new flag's author is seen by giving the flag its lab toggle, which ADR-187 A1 already requires, or by registering its SwarmSynth patch. **The limit:** an engine flag with neither is not seen.
3. **L9, new: the entries' order does not matter.** Every rule's findings, and each flag's L5 reading, are compared between the ledger as written and the ledger reversed.
4. **C3 runs for every default-0 built flag** (it ran for the last one in the file). An engine defaulting the flag ON must be rejected by L3, AND by L4 wherever an all-off scenario can hear the flag (L5's own reading). That is stricter than before for D1 and D2, the same for D3, and for D6 and D7, which no all-off scenario reaches, L3 is what holds a flipped default. # Round 4 (2026-10-13) — the release rule corrected, and the branch brought up to `main`

- **Queue item:** ROADMAP B459 (draft PR #1079). The lead's round-4 message of 2026-10-13, on round 3's open question 1.
- **Why:** the release rule as round 3 built it ("a stolen note keeps its gate; an off after the note has started is an ordinary off") had a step, which round 3 measured: an off 239 samples after the take kept a gate of 239 samples, an off at 240 or 241 left a gate of 0 or 1. The lead replaced the rule.

## The rule as it now stands (PROPOSED by the lead, the human's to ratify at merge)

**Everything about a stolen note is late by its wait, its release included, whenever the key is released.** A note that waited W samples before it started has every note-off for it take effect W samples after it arrives: while it still waits, on the sample it starts, or any time after. So the sounding gate equals the played gate.

As built, in the C++ and the JS together:

- `late` on the voice is how many samples late the note now on it started (W, the note's own wait: the fade's length, or what was left of a fade it attached to). `startVoice` sets it 0, `startWaiting` sets it. A note on a free voice has 0 and is released at once, by the line that was there before D7.
- An off while the note waits is remembered with its distance from the take, as in round 3, and takes effect that long after the start: W after it arrived.
- An off after the note has started is counted down on the voice (`offIn` = `late`) and takes effect when the count ends; the render cuts itself there. A second off before then changes nothing.
- A panic, a `polyMode` change or a new take of the voice cancels a pending off. `retune` is unaffected.
- **To the tiers:** a waiting note whose off has arrived is a releasing tail (M1, unchanged). A late note that has started and whose off has arrived but not yet taken effect is still gated and sounding, so it is a held voice until its release takes effect. That is the lead's reading, and the code needs no other: the voice law reads the gate, and the gate drops when the release takes effect.
- **JS only:** the oracle's poly note-off line (`razor-core.js` :433) is repeated in the composed engine's `noteOff`, because the oracle's own cannot be told to pass a late voice by. Mono and legato still call the oracle's.

Flag off is untouched: `late` is 0 on every voice unless `stealFade` has been on.
