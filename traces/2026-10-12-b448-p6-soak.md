# b448-p6-soak — the long-run soak of the horde 2 engine, a streaming read for the harness, nine controls and two nightly entries

- **Queue item:** B448 (Blind-Spot Armor), phase 2, package P6, ruled in ADR-209. Proposal: `docs/strategy/blind-spot-armor-phase2.md` section 1 row R5, section 3 row P6. Base branch: `armor2-wave2-wiring` (PR #1061), which carries the three comment markers this package inserts under.
- **Why:** row R5 had nothing on the engine: "No 4-hour soak; nothing on the engine". Its exit condition asks for "a nightly 4-hour soak per enrolled engine [that] holds level, DC, spectral-centroid and pitch bounds, its drift control fires, and the receipt is fresh". This package holds level, DC, non-finite samples, counter wraps and silence. It does **not** hold spectral centroid or pitch (`tools/patchspace/metrics.mjs` has neither, package P9 adds them, and the proposal forbids a second metrics library), so **row R5 stays partial**.
- **What changed:**
  - `h2/harness/harness.mjs`: `runStream(jobs, onChunk, opt)` and `renderStream(req, onChunk, opt)` read a render as it is written, through a named pipe. Every existing export is unchanged (the selftest and the parameter fuzz are green on it).
  - `tools/h2_soak_check.mjs` (new, `WIRED: ./verify full`): the soak.
  - `h2/harness/control_soak.h` (new) and seven rows in `h2/harness/adapters.h`, under the three P6 markers.
  - `tools/nightly/jobs.json`: two entries at the end, `soak-h2-long` (floor 899) and `soak-h2-extreme` (floor 149).
  - `verify`: one statement under `# [harness gate: P6 soak, the short local cell]`, as the brief gave it.
- **Five things a reader must know before trusting it:**
  1. **Windows are compared like with like because the note stream repeats.** One phrase (a drone held for the whole run, a long hold, two chords, a note struck four times, a note held over the boundary, singles) is drawn from the seed and played again every period; a window is exactly one period. `control/soak-steady`, played the same phrase, reads a level drift of 0.002 dB.
  2. **Level and DC are read on the pre-limiter bus, at a gain that keeps it readable.** The engine ends in tanh; near the rail a decibel of drift barely moves the output. Gain is read only by the limiter's two lines (the parameter fuzz holds that on every run), so a lower gain leaves the engine's state the same sample for sample. A bus above 8 or on the rail in a judged window is red, not quietly judged.
  3. **The real engine reaches a counter wrap only in one cell, one night in three.** `composed/long-192` renders four hours at 192 kHz, past 2^31 frames. The rule itself is proven on every run by two adapters whose own counters start 51.3 s short of 2^31 and 2^32.
  4. **Four hours at the extreme settings is not in the nightly.** The heavy cells render at about twice real time on the dev Mac when quiet; four hours of one is about 5.7 hours of a hosted runner at the assumed speed. The nightly holds four hours of three affordable cells and forty minutes of three extreme ones, in two rotations of three nights.
  5. **The night number comes from `date` in the job line.** The tool reads no clock; `--night N` picks cell N modulo 3 and the same N gives the same run. It is the one place a clock touches this package, and it chooses which cell runs, never a verdict.

- **What the soak found:**
  - **No drift, no non-finite sample, no wrap fault, in any cell.** The four-hour cells' first and last quarters agree within 0.012 dB in level.
  - **`extreme-loud` cycles.** In forty minutes its last quarter sat 0.17 dB under its first, so it was run for four hours: the level moves by about 0.13 dB with a period near one hour and returns (medians of twelve parts, L: -6.816 -6.938 -6.875 -6.828 -6.939 -6.848 -6.840 -6.940 -6.839 -6.854 -6.940 -6.813). Its DC also wanders ten times further than any other cell's (0.0045 of the level against 0.00045). Not a fault by any band here; it is why the level and DC bands are as wide as they are.
  - **The known issue (a held note at sustain 0 is never freed), measured outside the tool.** One note of the defaults patch with `S` 0 and `D` 10 ms, held 300 s at 48 kHz: 12.03 CPU s, against 2.71 when the key is released after 1 s. The silent voice costs a full voice for as long as the key is down. From 10 s in, the bus peaks at about 2.5e-322 in every stretch to the end and about 94 % of the output samples are non-zero subnormal numbers: without flush-to-zero the voice computes on them for ever. With `D` 4000 ms the level falls about 4.3 decades every 10 s, reaches the subnormal range after 700 to 800 s, and stays there. Over four hours in `long-min` (every held voice in that state) the level reads the same in all 899 windows to 0.001 dB.
  - **The harness does not render in the mode the shell will.** `adapter_composed.h` sets no flush-to-zero; ADR-211 rules it on in the shell. So the fuzz and the soak judge subnormal behaviour the shell will not have. On arm64 that costs little; on an x86 runner the cost of subnormal arithmetic is not measured and can be large, which is why `long-min` is not in the nightly rotation.
- **Alternatives rejected:**
  - *`/dev/fd/3` on an ordinary child pipe for the streaming read.* Works on macOS; Node's child pipes are sockets and Linux refuses to open a socket by path.
  - *Reading the named pipe through a non-blocking `net.Socket`.* On macOS the end of file never arrived (tried, 2026-10-11).
  - *A step measure from the half second each side of a wrap.* Read up to 1 dB on a healthy engine; the period before against the period after reads within 0.24 dB.
  - *A click window of half a second.* Any note-on in it masks the click; 4096 frames around the wrap does not.
  - *All six nightly cells every night.* About 5 hours of a hosted runner at the assumed speed.
  - *Four hours of the minimum corner in the nightly.* See the flush-to-zero point above.

- **Open questions:**

  - The hosted runner's speed is assumed (three times the dev Mac's quiet CPU time), not measured. At five times, `long-192` and `extreme-max` have about ten minutes to spare in a two-hour job.
  - `tools/nightly/run.py` runs every Linux entry in the draft workflow's one 120-minute job. The two soak entries are 96 to 121 minutes together at the assumed speed, and the fuzz's entry expects two to three hours by its own header: the night does not fit. The workflow is the human's.

  - The `date` in the two job lines is a design choice the lead may want otherwise (three entries a rotation, or the workflow's run number).
