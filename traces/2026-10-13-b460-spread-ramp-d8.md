## The keys: which ramp, which do not, and why

Eighteen values, nine per blade (`kSpreadKeys`; the JS's `srGet`).

| value | fields | with the flag on | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| position | `cOff`, `cOff2` | ramped, the shortest way round | a blade reads it modulo one cycle |
| cut offset, cut ratio | `kAdd`, `kMul`, `kAdd2`, `kMul2` | ramped while Quantize is off; SET while it is on | under `kq` the law itself steps them (a whole-number offset, a slot of the rule's list) |
| rotation rate | `rotOff`, `rotOff2` | ramped | a rate the render integrates; ramped after Rotate spread's sign is applied |
| width, depth, index | `wMul`, `dAdd`, `iMul` and blade 2's | ramped | continuous |
| band-limit r | `mr`, `mr2` | ramped | continuous in the member's frequency and shape |
| band-limit normaliser | `mn`, `mn2` | ramped as a pair with r | asin(r); see PROPOSED 3 |
| `mor`, `kEff` | | not touched | no blade reads them |

## PROPOSED, each the human's to ratify (the records give "the member spread values ramp across the tick" and no more)

1. **A position travels the shortest way round** (the brief's own example, as the lab's morph and D6's lead). Its starting value is first moved by whole cycles onto the target's branch, which no blade can hear, so it cannot wander. At Position spread 1 under spread law 3 the seam (a lead passing half a cycle) is then no movement at all, as with the flag off.
2. **Cut is set under Quantize.** Ramping it would sweep a quantized rate through unquantized ones for 0.67 ms at each change. Alternative not taken: ramp it anyway.
3. **The normaliser's target under the law's threshold is r itself.** The law writes `mn` 1 where r is at or under 1e-4, where the wave is a plain sine and reads no normaliser. A linear ramp between 1 and asin(r) would take the member's level to nearly zero on the way (r 0.005 over a normaliser of 0.5). With r as the target the pair follows the chord of asin from the origin, and the level stays at or under 1: it dips by 1 - r/asin(r) of the other end on a tick that starts or ends at the threshold (arithmetic: 0.14 % to r 0.09, 4.5 % to 0.5, 32 % to 0.995), and less between two values above it. The field then holds r, not 1, under the threshold with the flag on; nothing reads it there (C++ `waveK` case 6, JS `wave` :23). Alternative not taken: asin of the ramped r every sample (exact; two asin calls per member per sample).
4. **Who is set, not ramped** (ADR-216 A1 item 4's rule for a lead): every value at a note's first `spread()` and at the first after the flag turns on; a member that has just joined (N raised); a member still waiting on onset scatter, which is silent and holds the law's own values until the first tick after it starts.
5. **A note-on's `spread()` on a voice already sounding is not a tick** (a mono retrigger; D6's rule for `couple()`): the law runs, so its draws keep their order, and the ramps stay as they stand. The band-limit of a retriggered note at a new pitch therefore reaches its value up to two ticks later than with the flag off.
6. **Spread law 3's lead reaches the blade through the ramp of the values it writes** (ADR-216 A1 item 5). The lead itself is not ramped a second time inside `spread()`; under D6 the law reads the lead under the frame, as before.
7. **The tick measure's ceiling, 1.0 dB** (twice the flag-on worst), and **the composition's bound, 1e-12** (about 1500 times its worst).

## "At a tick boundary": the set, and its reason

Defined by what a step of the law can reach, from the engine's code, not by where a jump was seen. The tick falls on every 32nd sample (position 0), and the law's values change there before any blade is evaluated. A blade's output leaves one internal step late, and a PolyBLEP's leading half is added to the step going out now (`engine.h` `stepBlade`: `o = m.prev + blepOut`).

- **os 1: positions 0 and 1.** The blade's new value arrives at position 1; a BLEP's leading half, computed from the new values, at position 0; after that the values hold still for the rest of the tick.
- **os 2: positions 0 to 6.** The step enters the decimation filter (two biquads, a fourth-order low-pass at 0.45 sr; `engine.h` `setOS`) at the tick sample's first internal step (the BLEP's half) or its second (the value). A scratch script ran the filter's own coefficients on a unit step and read the output's move per position:

  | enters at | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | the first internal step | 0.364 | 0.792 | -0.196 | 0.046 | -0.002 | -0.008 | 0.007 | -0.004 |
  | the second | 0.068 | 0.781 | 0.259 | -0.164 | 0.084 | -0.038 | 0.015 | -0.005 |

  The set is every position at which either row moves by at least 1 % of its largest move: 0 to 6 (position 7 is 0.6 % and 0.7 %).

The detector names an event by its largest step, so a law's step is named at position 1 nearly always (every flag-off jump measured so far). The wider sets are judged so that one named a sample early or late is not let through. The price is stated in the check's header: a jump of the noise blade's falls inside the set by chance about 2 times in 32 at os 1 and 7 in 32 at os 2, and would turn the row red; it is not excused. The 1 % line is a choice (PROPOSED): 5 % would give 0 to 4, 10 % would give 0 to 4 too; no jump of the engine's measured in this work (the eight-seed sweep's, flag on or off, pad or twin) stands between positions 2 and 6 at os 2 or at position 0 at either os, so the choice moved no reading.
