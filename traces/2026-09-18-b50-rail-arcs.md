# b50-rail-arcs — every skip edge on the matrix rail arcs, so no two ribbons share a path

- **Queue item:** B50, the rail's arc rule ("RAIL: SKIP EDGES MUST ARC").
  *Provenance note:* the brief quotes that paragraph verbatim, but the labelled
  paragraph is not on `origin/main`'s ROADMAP at `a2f49d3` — what IS there is
  B50 phase 1b (a), "…skip edges arcing over", and the human's report of
  2026-09-18: *"all rails from SRC and along the main diagonal hide behind each
  other in a straight line, making it a little hard to see more complicated
  routings. I feel like any time a slot is skipped, it should be represented
  with a curved rail so I can see all of them at the same time."* Built against
  the brief's text; flagged to the lead rather than assumed (see Open questions).

- **Why:** Only SLOT→SLOT skips arced. Every Src→Slot edge, and every terminal
  slot's OUT edge, was drawn flat at `lift = 0` — so on a one-source rack
  Src→Slot 3 lay on the rail directly on top of Src→Slot 1, Slot 1→Slot 2 and
  Slot 2→Slot 3, and a Slot 1 that went straight out lay on the whole chain.
  Measured on the pre-change file: 63.6 px of shared path between two ribbons
  that are supposed to be two different signal routes.

## What changed (one file, `src/gui/gui2.html`, `drawMatrixGraph` and its hit test)

**The rule is written against HOPS, not against the edge's KIND.** The rail is
one node line — Src (all sources share column 0) · Slot 1…Slot n · OUT — and an
edge's hops is the distance along it. One hop is straight (it *is* the rail);
more than one arcs. Kinds no longer each carry their own lift expression, which
is what let two of the four be flat by omission.

| edge | hops | lift level (hops − 1) |
|---|---|---|
| Src → Slot t | t + 1 | t |
| Slot f → Slot t | t − f | t − f − 1 |
| Slot t → OUT (terminal) | slots − t | slots − t − 1 |
| Src → OUT (dry path) | slots + 1 | slots ← the tallest arc on the pane |

**Geometry constants.** `LIFT = 18·V` per skipped node; `LIFT_CAP = midY − 12·V`
(apex + half a pill + margin); `liftK = min(1, LIFT_CAP / (LIFT · slots))`. At
the four slots the rack has today the ladder is 18/36/54/72 px against a cap of
73 px, so `liftK = 1` and nothing is scaled. The previous constant was 30·V with
a per-edge `Math.min` clamp; 18 is what lets the *tallest possible* edge fit
rather than the tallest one that happened to be drawn.

**The cap is structural, not value-dependent** — computed from `slots` (the
dry path's span), never from the tallest edge currently non-zero. Otherwise an
arc would change height when a *neighbouring* cell was edited and the picture
would stop being readable as "how many slots does this jump".

**Cap decision, as the brief asks to be stated: the ladder COMPRESSES, the box
does not grow.** When the cap binds (it starts to at five slots) every arc
scales by the same `liftK`: the ordering survives and nothing clips. Growing the
canvas was rejected twice over — the height is a CSS rule (`#mxGraph
{ height:170px }`, the same 170 at the pane's 280 px min-width) outside this
painter's scope, and a painter that writes its own height feeds the
`ResizeObserver` watching it: paint → resize → paint.

**Above/below alternation, only where it is needed** (acceptance (c)): edges are
collected before any is drawn, then bucketed by `hops @ apex column`. A bucket of
one arcs above, as the demo does; a bucket of two goes above/below; a third and
fourth step out by a further 35 % each (clamped to the same cap), so the rule
survives the source count B23 increment 3 brings. A ZERO edge is never collected
— an invisible ribbon that consumed a lane would push a visible one to the wrong
side of the rail for no reason a reader could see.

**Everything derived follows the new geometry.** `my` is the cubic at t = 0.5,
i.e. the true apex (`(y1+y2)/2 + 0.75·lift` for control points at `y+lift`), and
it is the one number the pill, the hit-test record and the hover all read — so
they cannot disagree. An arc's pill now sits ON its apex: the ladder's rungs are
one LIFT apart, so a pill standing off its own arc would be punched through the
arc one rung above it and label the wrong ribbon. A straight edge keeps the 16 px
standoff it has always had. Line weight, the terminal-path ink/faint split
(`reaches`), the dashed inverted edge and the `.mxhi` coupling are untouched.

## The overlap proof (acceptance (e)) — measured, with two must-fail controls

`mxarc_probe.mjs` (session scratchpad, `scratchpad/b50-arcs/`; see Open
questions) extracts the mx block by exact anchors, runs it in a `vm` against a
2D context that RECORDS `beginPath`/`moveTo`/`bezierCurveTo`/`stroke`, and
measures the geometry the shipped painter emits. Nothing in it restates the lift
rule, or it would agree with itself (L0032).

- **Metric:** each cubic sampled at 400 points; two ribbons *share path* where
  their centrelines are within **3.1 px** — the widest stroke this painter draws
  ((0.9 + 2.2·|v|)·V at |v| = 1), i.e. the distance at which two ribbons stop
  being two lines on screen. Shared LENGTH is the arc length of A inside B, taken
  as the max of both directions.
- **Budget N = 25 px, justified:** ribbons that MEET at a card port must coincide
  at that port and two arcs of different span CROSS — neither is the defect the
  rule names. What it forbids is one ribbon lying ALONG another. 25 px is under
  half the shortest ribbon the rail can draw (one hop, 57 px at the probe's
  600 px width) and under a card's width (30–50 px), the smallest object on the
  pane: a merged stretch is always shorter than the smallest thing a reader can
  see, so no edge can hide inside another.

| run | worst shared path | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| subject — 1 source, split topology + dry path | **17.8 px** (a crossing of two equal-span arcs) | GREEN, exit 0 |
| subject — 2 sources (increment 3's shape, alternation live) | **17.8 px** | GREEN, exit 0 |
| **control** — ladder planted flat (`LIFT = 0`, anchor asserted 1×) | **272.8 px** | RED, exit 1 |
| **control** — `origin/main` file, unmutated | **63.6 px** | RED, exit 1 |

The second control is the human's report as a number: on the pre-change file the
Src→Slot 3 ribbon runs 63.6 px inside Slot 1→Slot 2 and 60.2 px inside Src→Slot 1.
Both controls fire; neither passes for the wrong reason (the plant's anchor is
asserted to match exactly once).

## Alternatives rejected

- **Growing `#mxGraph`'s height when the cap binds.** Out of scope (CSS, not this
  painter) and self-defeating: the ResizeObserver on this canvas turns a height
  write into a repaint loop. Compression keeps the ordering and cannot clip.
- **A per-kind lift expression** (add one to the Src→Slot loop, one to the OUT
  loop). That is the shape that produced the defect: four call sites, two of them
  flat because nobody wrote the term. One hops → lift rule, four spans.
- **Pills kept 16 px off every arc.** At an 18 px rung a standoff pill is punched
  through the arc ABOVE it, so it labels the wrong ribbon. Apex for arcs, standoff
  for straight edges.
- **Alternating every skip edge above/below.** The brief's (c) restricts it to
  equal span + same apex column, and so does this: a lone skip always arcs above,
  which keeps the default reading the demo's.

## Open questions

1. **The acceptance paragraph is not in the tree.** `origin/main`'s ROADMAP at
   `a2f49d3` has no "RAIL: SKIP EDGES MUST ARC" paragraph in B50; the rule was
   quoted in the dispatch brief. Built to the brief, which matches phase 1b (a)
   and the human's words — but the lead owns writing it into B50, and until then
   this trace is the only tracked copy.
2. **The probe is not a gate.** Landing it would need a new `tools/labharness/`
   file and a `./verify` line, both out of this brief's scope. It lives in the
   session scratchpad with its controls (`run.sh`, `proof.txt`).
3. **The ladder's base (18 px) is fixed to today's 170 px canvas.** The cap makes
   a five-slot rack legal, but arcs then compress to ~14.6 px a rung; if B45's
   roster lands a rack that deep, the honest fix is a taller pane, which is a CSS
   decision, not a painter one.
4. **Three or more edges of one span over one column** (three sources feeding one
   slot) alternate with a 35 % step that is clamped at the cap, so lanes 2 and 4
   coincide at the tallest level. Untestable today — the rack has one source —
   and named here rather than discovered later.
